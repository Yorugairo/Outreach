# Narrative Worlds and Evidence Grammar

## Summary

Preserve Steel and Paper's illustrated editorial identity, use Wealth Logic's concrete explanation, and borrow Bravos' progressive evidence development. This is an editorial formula to test, not a measured retention guarantee or a mandate to average the channels' styles.

Governing principle: make the story easy to follow and its reasoning possible to inspect. Evidence supports the narrative and makes complexity visible; it is not a competing presentation or a reason to interrupt the viewing experience. Operator endorsed this direction on September20; the production proofs remain subject to existing asset and pilot gates.

## Intent And Acceptance

- Plan at least ceil(runtime_seconds / 10) distinct narrative/art plate candidates: 6 for 60 seconds, 18 for 180 seconds, 48–72 for 8–12 minutes. Updated by operator September20 from the earlier12-second floor. Candidates are reviewable art compositions, not just prompts, crops or alternate camera angles. Existing suitable art counts; unused candidates are allowed.
- Separate candidate coverage, chosen compositions, internal visual events and screen-time shares. None substitutes for another.
- Every selected beat has a claim, viewer question, visual mode, focal subject, evidence custody, motion purpose, transition handoff and exit reason.
- Narrative images remain visible when their story matters. Data receives full space when comparison matters. No compulsory chart overlay on every illustration.
- Preserve approved script and sources. No data generation, speculative figures or implied causal transfers introduced through animation.
- The video is self-contained: viewers can understand the argument without leaving to consult references. Full citations normally live in the description; internal claim-to-source custody remains complete regardless of visible citation placement.

Use this as a reasoning loop, not four mandatory shots. Open with a person, tangible object or striking contrast; explain the relevant change; show source-bound proof; return to what that means for the person or object. The return must add something, not replay an unchanged plate.

Each editorial beat = one complete idea + one primary visual task + one meaningful state change + a motivated handoff.

### Editorial continuity and attention

Narrative leads; evidence answers the question the narrative has created. Ask for one primary attention task at a time: do not require simultaneous chart reading and interpretation of detailed narrative action. Grow evidence when its comparison matters; reduce, shift or remove it when the human consequence takes over. Preserve the relevant document, object, datum or spatial relationship so the viewer need not reconstruct the reference after each move.

Use the existing effects catalogue as editorial vocabulary, not decoration:

- Re-scale/grow: grant reading space to the item whose meaning now matters; keep readable labels and honest chart scales.
- Shift/recompose: make space for a related item while retaining the reference needed to compare them.
- Throw: visibly relocate an item or close its argument, with a motivated destination or exit.
- Melt to ball and throw off screen: clearly retire a completed idea and free the field; do not erase evidence the next sentence still needs.
- Stop-motion and whirl: articulate an action or a meaningful transformation using the established house treatment; do not use them to camouflage unrelated changes.

Short-format recipes must be re-authored for horizontal composition, sightlines, caption clearance and reading size, not merely stretched. Choose transitions by the relationship they preserve or conclude. The new capability is harmonious evidence and world staging, not a mandate to add more effects.

### Source placement: self-contained viewing, inspectable provenance

Separate the evidence the viewer needs to SEE from the citation they may choose to CHECK. Charts, comparisons, pertinent excerpts and visual mechanisms stay in the video when they explain the argument. Bibliographies, full URLs and routine source credits can stay outside it.

- Description: organize full source links by chapter or claim, including dataset/table, observation window or publication date, and relevant calculation notes. Maintain exact bindings to the internal evidence ledger. Do not use a generic link dump as a substitute for claim attribution.
- On screen: keep units, axes, period/snapshot date, population/scope and estimate/illustration labels when needed to interpret the visual honestly. These are meaning, not citation clutter. A label may be contextual rather than repeated on every frame if its meaning stays unambiguous.
- Show attribution when it is itself part of the argument, such as a named institution's claim or a quoted source; preserve any required credit/licensing treatment. A documentary excerpt is an editorial choice, not a mandatory source-card interruption.
- No requirement for a permanent source footer on every chart. Moving routine citations to the description must not remove provenance from the ledger or make a misleading chart appear clean.
- Acceptance: a viewer can follow the complete explanation without opening a link, while a curious viewer can locate the supporting source for each material claim from the description.

### Mode selection

1. NARRATIVE: viewer needs to feel or understand the situation. Art owns the frame. Evidence optional, restrained and never over the key action.
2. MOUNTED EVIDENCE: context and a specific fact must coexist. Mount/dock evidence in a world with a clear read surface. If it covers the reason for the image or needs unreadably small type, use another mode.
3. FULL LEDGER: viewer needs to compare quantities, trace relationships or inspect a mechanism. Ledger owns the frame; narrative props may enter only when they clarify the comparison. A ledger can carry a mechanism, not only a chart.

Choose by the sentence's job, not by which asset is available. Any mode can follow any other. A source document can grow from a world into a full ledger; a ledger datum can return as the workshop's bill. Never animate money between accounts unless the underlying claim supports that flow.

### Coverage and pacing formulas

- Coverage floor: C = ceil(T/10), with at least one claim-linked candidate for each planned approximately 10-second interval. Uneven actual shot lengths are expected.
- New art required = max(0, C - suitable distinct existing candidates). Variants that change the depicted action may count; recolors and crops do not.
- Runtime accounting: T = narrative-only time + mounted-evidence time + full-ledger time, assigning exactly one dominant mode at each moment. Report the shares after authoring; do not force an arbitrary universal percentage.
- Before leaving a world, ask whether the next idea can be built there legibly. Before staying, ask whether the world still explains the current sentence. Either answer can justify a cut.
- Life on stills: meaningful prop/cast action or reveal first; selective local depth second. Camera travel establishes a relationship, arrives, then settles for reading. Breathing/pulsing is not a substitute for information.
- Transition hierarchy: carry the same document/object/value when semantically continuous; transform compatible charts while preserving scale truth; use melt/page/world transitions at conceptual changes; retain hard cuts for genuine contrasts and punctuation. No decorative ban or quota.
- Author the data build once. Do not repeatedly erase/redraw a completed chart to simulate activity. Highlight the referenced datum or label, not the entire mark.
