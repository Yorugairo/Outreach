# 30 — Voice Source Material

Input for `brand-voice`. Everything here is evidence of how the operator
actually writes and what he rejects, gathered from working sessions rather
than asserted as preference. Corrections carry more signal than approvals:
an approval says "acceptable", a rejection says where the line is.

---

## 1. Biography — the two-altitude view

The non-replicable asset. Three facts, all LinkedIn-verifiable, in the
operator's own framing:

- **JPMorgan — business risk analyst / product manager.** Measured downside
  professionally; watched high earners carry zero margin, up close.
- **Dispensary owner.** An all-cash business. Employed the people finance
  YouTube never talks about. Counted the register.
- **Product manager building with AI.** Systems beat intentions; money is a
  product with a spec.

**Correction on the record (2026-08-24):** an earlier draft said he *traded*
at JPMorgan. He did not. Corrected to "worked in risk."

**The decision that followed matters more than the correction.** Offered the
option of a small truth-bend for a stronger claim, he declined:

> "the truth bend on that might not be worth it since i'll probably link to my
> Linkedin and do an intro-story with my face on the video even though the
> channel will be faceless. I think that credibility can give me an edge."

Voice rule: **verifiability is the differentiator, not a constraint on it.**
Never inflate a credential for punch. The channel is faceless; the operator
is not anonymous.

---

## 2. Worked example — a rejected hook and its replacement

The single most useful artefact here, because both versions survive.

**Rejected (v1).** Operator's verdict:

> "that hook is actually terrible and it's written like the entry to a book
> not a youtube video/ movie."

**Accepted (v5):**

> She earns half of what he does. She retires first. He's a banker in
> Manhattan, counting his bonus. She's a budtender, counting the till in my
> dispensary — I own it, and I worked in risk at JPMorgan. The difference
> isn't discipline and it isn't the salary — it fits on a single receipt, and
> by the end of this video you'll calculate yours.

What changed, and these are the rules:

| Rejected | Accepted |
| --- | --- |
| atmosphere first | **first sentence is the grab** |
| past/expository | **present tense, viewer-facing** |
| paragraph-shaped | **shot list — one cut per sentence** |
| biography as credential up front | **biography as the twist after the paradox** |
| closes an idea | **opens a loop with a dated payout** |

Third rule, stated separately and worth keeping verbatim in spirit: *judge by
ear against synthesized audio, never on the page.* A hook that reads well and
sounds wrong is wrong.

---

## 3. The retention clock (operator doctrine)

Governs every beat, not just the open:

- **0-3s** — the grab is the FIRST sentence. No atmosphere first.
- **by 10s** — answer it.
- **by 30s** — promise the payout for continuing.
- then **repeat per beat**: hook, prove it, promise more, deliver.

Operator's phrasing: *"We have to hook them, show them its worth it, promise
more, then deliver; over and over again."*

---

## 4. Characters are people from real worlds, not demographics

> "we need more of my own voice included... put these 2 characters into niches
> of people instead of generic people"

So: not "someone earning $95k" but **the banker** and **the budtender** —
people the narrator has actually stood next to. Concrete occupation over
income bracket. The claim to have been in both rooms *is* the hook.

---

## 5. Register — YouTube, not essay

The humanizer discipline applies, but bent for the medium:

> "use /humanizer but with a curve: This is for Youtube so we need consistent
> hooks, viralization, callbacks to keep users engaged, even when we're
> grounding in fact. it shouldn't read the same way as my Trade union post."

Meaning: concrete nouns, varied cadence, active voice, no stock AI phrasing —
**plus** cold-open hooks, open loops, planted callbacks, second person, and
specific numbers. Spoken register, not editorial prose.

---

## 6. Entity seeding

Name one bigger adjacent authority per video — passed authority plus
topic-graph adjacency. Operator's reasoning on Mr Money Mustache:

> "bringing in mr money moustache into it is a good play too, algorithms might
> connect them, he's a more popular person in the field, and it adds some
> passed authority."

Cite the primary source when the authority is invoked as evidence.

---

## 7. Anti-patterns — observed rejections

Each of these was produced, shown, and rejected in session:

- **Opening on black with text.** *"you can't start your youtube video on a
  black screen."* Open on a plate.
- **Writing that reads like a book intro.** See §2.
- **Inflated or unverifiable credentials.** See §1.
- **Generic characters** where specific ones were available. See §4.
- **Verbal tics copied from a reference.** Alicia's "I want you to picture…"
  was deliberately NOT copied while her structure was.
- **Production scaffolding visible to the viewer** — internal deck names,
  approval states, slot indices. Provenance belongs in the manifest.
- **Padding for runtime.** Match length to the story.

---

## 8. Operator register in his own words

Sampled from working messages, as evidence of natural cadence: lowercase and
unpunctuated when thinking aloud; blunt verdicts ("that hook is actually
terrible"); reasons attached to decisions rather than assertions alone;
hedges marked explicitly ("i think", "maybe", "not sure"); and self-correction
in the open ("i was probably wrong about the sweep, it was better before").

That last habit is the one to preserve in the written voice: **state the
change and the reason, don't perform the reversal.**

The profile is the artefact scripts and work orders reference by path, so this
material stops being re-derived by hand each session.
