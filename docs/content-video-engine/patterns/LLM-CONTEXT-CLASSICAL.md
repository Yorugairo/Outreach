ACOUSTIC NARRATIVE ARCHITECTURE (LLM KNOWLEDGE CONTEXT)

1. BROADCAST & RADIO WRITING MECHANICS

* Cognitive Constraint: Auditory text is real-time, linear, and ephemeral; requires low working-memory load.
* Sentence Length: Target 15–16 words average. Single-idea declarative sentences only.
* Voice & Syntax:
   * Strict Active Voice: Subject-Verb-Object (S-V-O).
   * Attribution-First Placement: Source precedes assertion (e.g., "The council decided X", not "X, decided the council").
   * Positive Phrasing & Plain Language: Eliminate abstract Latinate verbs, complex conjunctions, and homophones/phonetic ambiguity.
* Terminal Stress Principle: Place the single most surprising, critical, or emotionally resonant word at the very end of the sentence.
* Address & Signposting:
   * Direct Address: Use direct second-person ("you") rather than collective descriptions.
   * Auditory Handrails: Explicit verbal transitions (signposts) indicating past context, current position, and next direction.

2. SCREENWRITING STRUCTURE & BEAT MACHINERY

* Robert McKee (Story Mechanics):
   * Hierarchy: Beat (action/reaction exchange shifting emotional charge) -> Scene -> Sequence -> Act -> Story.
   * Expectation-Reality Gap: Narrative movement occurs only when an action yields an unexpected environmental result, opening a gap that forces a deeper, riskier choice.
   * Principle of Antagonism: Narrative quality is constrained by forces of opposition (systemic, internal, environmental).
   * Archetype vs. Stereotype: Wrap universal human experiences in unique, specific expressions.
* John Truby (Organic Story Architecture):
   * 7 Structural Steps: 1. Weakness/Need -> 2. Desire -> 3. Opponent -> 4. Plan -> 5. Battle -> 6. Self-Revelation -> 7. New Equilibrium.
   * Designing Principle: Overall organizing strategy (Story Process + Original Execution).
   * Moral Argument: Theme expressed through structural action and dilemmas (choices between two desirable goods or two undesirable evils).
* Blake Snyder (Save the Cat Machinery):
   * Midpoint Pivot: Halfway hinge acting as false peak or false collapse; elevates stakes, ends exploratory phase, shifts protagonist from proactive to reactive.

3. SPEECHWRITING RHETORIC & ACOUSTIC CADENCE

* James Humes (Strategic Silence / Power Pauses):
   * Pre-Opener Pause: Silence before first word to command attention.
   * Pre-Key Pause: Silence immediately preceding crucial thesis or revelation.
   * Post-Key Pause: Silence following major point to allow cognitive absorption.
* Syntactical Patterns & Cadence:
   * Tricolon (Rule of Three): Triadic grouping (setup, extension, resolution/subversion) for pattern recognition.
   * Anaphora: Repetition of initial clause phrases to anchor attention while varying trailing context.
   * Acoustic Wave: Alternating rhythmic, longer setup sentences with short, punchy conclusions. Use assonance and alliteration for key phrase anchoring.

4. DOCUMENTARY VO & NARRATIVE NONFICTION CRAFT

* Ira Glass Dual-Engine Model:
   * Building Block 1: Anecdote = Chronological action sequence raising continuous unresolved questions ("attentional bait").
   * Building Block 2: Reflection = Interruption of action to extract thematic, philosophical, or systemic meaning.
   * Execution Rule: Continuously loop Anecdote (bait) and Reflection (insight).
* Macro Structures:
   * Martini Glass: Initial anecdotal hook -> Chronological body -> Broad thematic reflection/coda.
   * Kabob Structure: Multiple discrete anecdotal vignettes skewered onto a central thematic thread.
* Audio-Visual Counterpoint:
   * Avoid visual tautology (narration echoing visuals). Voiceover track provides subtext, internal motive, or ironic contrast to visual imagery.

5. ANCIENT ORAL MECHANICS

* Ring Composition (Chiasmus): Symmetrical structural framing where opening themes are revisited in reverse order post-climax (A-B-C-X-C'-B'-A') to create cognitive completeness without visual cues.
