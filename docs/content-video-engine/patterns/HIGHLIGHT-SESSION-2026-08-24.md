6. (b11) The Terminal Stress Principle
7. (b12) In oral composition, the position of a word within a sentence dictates its psychological impact6. Scriptwriters utilize the Terminal Stress Principle, which dictates that the most critical, surprising, or emotionally resonant word must be placed at the very end of the sentence6. Because human auditory processing anchors most strongly to the final sound before a micro-pause, ending a sentence with weak prepositions, qualifiers, or subordinate clauses dissipates narrative momentum6.

11. (b20) Narrative progression is fundamentally propelled by the Gap between Expectation and Result12. Narrative momentum breaks down when an action produces the exact result expected by the audience14. McKee asserts that dramatic tension lives exclusively in the gap between what a character or narrator expects to happen when taking an action and the unexpected reality returned by the environment12. This gap forces the audience to pay closer attention, raises a dramatic question, and compels the narrative voice to adapt by taking a riskier, deeper action12. Every turning point in a high-performing script operates by opening a gap, shifting the value charge from positive to negative or vice versa, and delivering insight as a reward for the viewer's attention12.

22. (b37) The human brain is an efficient pattern-matching engine24. Speechwriting leverages this cognitive reality through the Rule of Three, or tricolon24. Triads create an acoustic rhythm that feels balanced, complete, and memorable24. The first item establishes a baseline, the second extends the pattern, and the third completes the structural unit—or, in comedic and high-impact rhetoric, subverts the established pattern to generate surprise24.
23. (b38) Complementing the triad is Anaphora—the deliberate repetition of a starting word or phrase across successive clauses23. Anaphora establishes a rhythmic acoustic beat that serves as an anchor for complex arguments23. By keeping the opening phrase constant, the listener’s cognitive processing burden is reduced, allowing them to focus entirely on the evolving conceptual information at the end of each clause6.

27. (b43) The Ira Glass Dual-Engine Model
28. (b45) The Anecdote serves as sequential attentional bait31. It consists of a sequence of actions arranged chronologically, detailing what happened in a step-by-step progression32. Human cognition is naturally drawn to sequential stories32. Crucially, an effective anecdote must constantly raise unresolved questions in the listener's mind, building momentum that compels the viewer to stay engaged to find the answer31.
29. (b46) The Reflection provides meaning generation31. An anecdote operating alone is merely a series of disconnected events2. To transform events into a compelling narrative, the writer must periodically interrupt the chronological action to deliver a moment of reflection that answers why the story matters31. The reflection extracts thematic, philosophical, or systemic lessons from the preceding anecdote2.
30. (b47) A script consisting entirely of reflection becomes an abstract, alienating lecture, whereas a script consisting entirely of anecdotes becomes a shallow list of events2. High-performing narrative scripts continuously alternate between these two engines, using the anecdote to capture attention and the reflection to deliver cognitive value31.

36. (b58) A powerful structural tool inherited from oral tradition is Ring Composition, also known as Chiasmus35. Ring composition is a symmetrical narrative structure where a series of themes or events introduced in the first half of a script are systematically revisited in reverse order during the second half, forming a closed structural loop35.
37. (b59) In a chiastic script structure, the narrative begins at an initial premise and moves inward through secondary contextual elements to reach a central, transformative thesis or revelation35. Once this central anchor is delivered, the script moves back outward, systematically echoing and resolving the initial elements in reverse order until returning to the opening theme35.
38. (b59) This structural symmetry provides the listener with profound cognitive satisfaction and an intuitive sense of narrative completion, anchoring complex information without relying on visual reminders35.
39. (b61) Robert McKee draws a vital distinction between archetypal storytelling and stereotypical storytelling13. A stereotypical script relies on superficial, culture-specific generalities and trendy visual formulas that decay quickly and fail to resonate outside a narrow audience segment13.

When scriptwriters ground their narratives in archetypal mechanics, they tap into deeply ingrained psychological patterns, capturing viewer attention regardless of current platform trends13.
