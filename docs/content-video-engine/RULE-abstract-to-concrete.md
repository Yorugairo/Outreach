# The plate throws the punch — abstract-to-concrete, 2026-09-04

Adopted from the doodle-channel master prompt's rule 5, which is the one genuinely
transferable idea in it:

> *"Translate abstract narration into concrete visuals — if the script says 'your body
> doesn't know the difference', show a confused stick figure looking at two identical
> objects; if it says 'millions of years', show a large hourglass."*

Operator's diagnosis of why we need it, which is sharper than the rule itself:

> *"Our scenes tend to be **background** scenes, scenes that match the narration
> setting; not **action** scenes that throw the punch. Our image generation is built
> around generating image scenery that looks cool, then filling the world with
> evidence."*

## The proof, from our own work

The Tokyo short's ring line is **already a physicalised metaphor, written**:

> *"Tokyo took a tea break. And left Washington holding an unfunded bar tab."*

An empty chair. A cooling cup. A bill on the table, unpaid. That is the shot.

**The plate I authored for it is `plate-05-tokyo-dawn-desk` — a desk at dawn.** We wrote
the metaphor and then illustrated the *setting it was spoken in*. That is the whole
defect in one shot, and it is mine from yesterday.

## The rule

> **A plate depicts what the sentence MEANS, not where it was said.**
> If the plate would still make sense with the narration muted, it is scenery. If it
> only makes sense *because* of the line, it is the punch.

Test on every shot: **cover the caption. Can you still tell what claim is being made?**
Scenery survives that test — which is the problem. A metaphor plate fails it, and that
failure is the evidence it is doing work.

## Frame types that carry a punch

Adapted from the source's rule 8, kept where it fits our register and dropped where it
does not:

| type | what it does | our use |
|---|---|---|
| **Personified mechanism** | an abstract force given a body | **the strongest one for us.** Doctrine says the opponent is a mechanism, never a person; a mechanism with a *body* is an antagonist you can see without blaming anybody. A hedge cost as a toll gate. A yield as a discount machine eating future dollars. |
| **Concept object** | one big object carrying the abstraction | "millions of years" → hourglass. Ours: "a trillion in Treasuries" → one absurd stack against one small figure. |
| **Before/after progression** | left-to-right change with an arrow | "hedging cost half what it did" — the toll price crossed out and halved, traveller still walking away |
| **Reaction** | a figure's posture IS the claim | our host's approved poses, used for meaning rather than presence |
| **Labelled diagram** | object + arrow + one word | already how our badges work |

Dropped: thought bubbles, globe-and-creatures, the doodle style itself. Different
channel, different register.

## What this unlocks with the ledger page — the operator's structural point

> *"It was hard to move from narration plate to narration plate while carrying charts.
> Now we can go from a metaphoric narration plate to the chart plate, and building that
> blank cream into the graph is the perfect way to fill those narration gaps too."*

This is the part that changes the pipeline, not just the art direction.

**Before:** two scenery plates in a row, with a chart docked over one of them. The chart
had to survive a plate change or die, which is what E25 had to legislate against.

**Now:** metaphor plate → **the page itself becomes the chart**. The ledger page (E22)
starts as blank cream, charcoal fills to the deckle, the line is drawn, the chart
builds. The plate does not get replaced — **it transforms into the evidence.**

And it lands exactly where the acoustic-gap finding says a transition should:

| LP phase | duration |
|---|---|
| ROLL | 0.7 s |
| SAVOR | 0.8 s |
| FIELD | 2.4 s |
| PUNCH | 0.5 s |
| BUILD | 3.0 s |
| **focus fires at** | **7.4 s** |

A cut has to complete inside the gap — 0.45–1.5 s, which is tight. **A build only has to
BEGIN there.** ROLL + SAVOR is 1.5 s: the page starts turning in the silence and the
chart draws under the next sentence, so the viewer sees the evidence arrive *while* the
claim is being made rather than after it.

That is the three findings meeting: cut on the gap (M13), the chart proves one sentence
and leaves (E25), and the plate is the punch (this rule).

## Execute at the PROP tier, not the world tier

Checked against Wealth Logic's own frames (`COMPOSITING-VERDICT.md`). Their frame for
"your own money is tied up" is: one character, a pile of coloured pens, a cash bundle,
five floating calendars — **four asset types on an empty white ground.** No world.

Our revised Tokyo table asks for six *new world plates* to carry six metaphors. Theirs
would ask for six *new props* against a ground we already own — and a prop is reusable
forever, where a world plate is spent on one shot.

> **Props compose. Worlds don't.**

The asset registry already separates `world` / `actor` / `prop` / `mechanism`. We have
been generating at the `world` tier out of habit, which is why every metaphor felt
expensive and why the plates drifted toward scenery: a fully-dressed scene has to be
*somewhere*, and "somewhere" is the setting the rule warns against.

A metaphor brief should therefore name **the actor's pose + the props + the ground**,
not a place. "Toll gate" is a prop and a pose, not a location.

**But not on white — on the page.** Their empty ground is their identity; ours is the
cream ledger (E22). Props get drawn on it in ink, and then the same sheet becomes the
chart. See `RULE-the-page-is-the-ground.md`: it is the same move in our own language,
and it makes metaphor→evidence a transformation rather than a cut.

## Consequences for the shot table

1. **Every narration plate gets a metaphor brief, not a setting brief.** The claim
   first, the world second. "What is the visual argument" before "where are we."
2. **Metaphor plate → ledger page is the default pair.** Not plate → plate → dock.
3. **A plate that survives the muted-caption test is flagged**, not automatically
   rejected — an establishing shot is legitimate once per episode, not thirteen times.

## What else is in that master prompt

Almost nothing for us. It is a stage-gated wizard (topic ideas → script → prompts →
metadata) for a 16:9 doodle channel, and our pipeline is stronger at every stage. Two
minor notes worth recording:

- **Its rule 7 — "hold scenes across consecutive timestamps, do not generate a brand
  new scene every 5 seconds"** — agrees with Wealth Logic's ~10 s holds and with our own
  E25 ceiling. Independent confirmation, nothing new.
- **It has no evidence discipline at all.** "Include at least 3 real named researchers"
  with no verification step, no source, no dossier. That is the thing we do better than
  any reference we have looked at, and it should not drift.
