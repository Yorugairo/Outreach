## 2. What the evidence settles for our plan

### 2.1 Channel breadth → lanes within one promise (decision affirmed)

Sam O'Nella proves single-channel breadth **when persona + art style is the product**; MinutePhysics
proves the spin-off discipline when the brand promise is topical. Our resolution (one audience
promise per channel, badge-coded lanes rotating within it — `02-CONTENT-STRATEGY.md` §2) is the
synthesis both precedents support. The operator's lane-rotation research (badges, playlist
shelves, predictable days) is how Sam-style breadth is operationalized without confusing the
watch-next graph.

### 2.2 The two growth lanes — and which is ours

- **Event-upload lane** (OverSimplified 3–4/yr; Jhanzou 13 videos → 1.5M subs): works only at
  spectacle quality; catalog compounds while dormant (Jhanzou +60K subs/yr asleep). Not our
  comparative advantage — we can't out-choreograph dedicated animators.
- **Volume-funnel lane** (Casual Geographic TikTok-batch; Historically's Shorts-led cold start):
  discovery through short-form volume, durability through long-form. **A programmatic pipeline's
  marginal cost is unbeatable exactly here.** Historically's curve is our planned motion in the
  wild: Shorts buy the audience (0→133K in 5 months), long-form converts it to durable
  subs/revenue — and their 2024 plateau is the warning that Shorts alone stall without long-form
  maturing behind them.
- Serious History adds the middle-path proof: **unbroken 2/month long-form with a franchisable
  formula** compounds from zero with no Shorts at all. Formula + cadence discipline substitutes
  for spectacle.

### 2.3 The voice finding (the plan's biggest caution, now with zero counterexamples)

Across all eight channels: **no AI-sounding narration anywhere in the success set.** The giants
are all creator-voiced comic personas; the emerging growers recruit human VAs (Serious History),
credit them (Jhanzou), or turn the human accent into the meme (Historically). An anonymous
synthetic voice has no observed success precedent in these niches.
Consequences (already in the docs): clone the operator's own voice (disclosure-exempt,
persona-bearing, ElevenLabs-Default-retirement-proof); put the persona in the writing (running
gags, catchphrases, recurring cast); treat voice A/B as a pilot-critical experiment (assumption
A5) — and if TTS reads as slop to early viewers, the fallback is operator-recorded VO, which the
pipeline supports natively.

### 2.4 Cold-start expectations (pre-commit these to calibrate the kill criteria)

- Plan for **5–12 months of obscurity** on merit formats; less only if the Shorts flywheel
  catches (Historically). Our pilot's 4–6 week window measures *retention quality*, not
  channel growth — growth verdicts before month 6 would be noise.
- **Finished units only.** PIERCE's 515 fragments → 45K subs vs Jhanzou's 13 finished films →
  1.5M is the starkest pair in the dataset. Nothing ships from our pipeline that isn't a
  complete, QC-passed piece. No WIP/teaser uploads.
- **Ship a repeatable promise, sequel the winners fast.** Serious History's franchise titles
  ("Most X in History" → "More X...") reliably re-pull 3–6M. Our series lanes are built for
  this: when a *Physics of Grappling* video wins, its sequel is a storyboard away — pipe speed
  is our advantage; use it on winners, not on volume for its own sake.
- **Viral ≠ subscribed without a promise:** PIERCE's 1.5M-view hit converted ~1.3% because
  nothing invited a return visit. Every video ends inside a lane (badge, series name, next-episode
  hook) so a hit has somewhere to deposit its audience.

### 2.5 Motion-quality bar warning

Stick-figure *fight* animation is Jhanzou territory: the genre's bar is choreography spectacle
built over months. We do not compete there. Our stick figures exist to carry **diagrams, jokes,
and mechanics** (closer to Sam O'Nella's deliberately crude drawings + strong writing) — the
JointLeverageScene explanations are the differentiator, and "animated BJJ biomechanics" remains
an empty format-niche intersection in this dataset. Positioning: information gain + persona, not
choreography.

### 2.6 Monetization layering (sequenced expectations)

Observed order: Patreon/Ko-fi at first traction → memberships → merch + brand sponsors in the
500K+ zone (Historically: World of Warships #ad; OverSimplified: NordVPN-as-recurring-character).
Ad RPM alone sustains nobody at low cadence. Our stack is stronger than the comparables' at the
bottom: the registry SaaS conversion node exists from day one — but the observed layering still
says: link a Patreon-equivalent early, design sponsor-friendly formats, and never lead with RPM.
Also: licensed mainstream music is a self-inflicted wound (PIERCE) — library/commissioned music
only, which our compositor config already enforces.

### 2.7 Operator-provided MKBHD / The Studio strategy input

Source: [How To Start A Million Subscriber YouTube Channel by MKBHD / The Studio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Puny-2wkMZA).
The notes below were supplied by the operator and have **not** been independently checked against
a transcript; they are decision inputs, not verified quotations or economic evidence.

- **Outlier theory:** compare a video's views with its channel baseline rather than treating raw
  view count as proof. **Trend theory:** translate a macro cultural topic into the channel's
  audience promise.
- **Inversion over imitation:** preserve the successful question or tension, then challenge its
  premise with an original, source-backed angle.
- **Skill stacking:** combine capabilities competitors rarely possess together; the combination,
  not generic production polish, is the defensible moat.
- **Pre-launch batching:** finish three to four videos before opening the public feedback loop.
  This doc set adopts a minimum buffer of three Gate-B-approved, packaged videos.
- **AI boundary:** AI is an execution tool; human perspective and judgment prevent regression
  toward generic output.
- **Brand and money:** a broad master identity can evolve while series playlists carry topical
  specificity; the source of sponsorship revenue affects audience trust.

The operator-provided CPM ranges are not added to the economics table without primary support.
The existing verified planning assumptions remain authoritative.
