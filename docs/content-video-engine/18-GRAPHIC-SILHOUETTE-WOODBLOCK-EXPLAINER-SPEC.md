## Purpose

This is an original visual language for history, sport culture, and timely
explainers. It combines a textured Combat Woodblock world with a simple,
high-contrast Graphic Silhouette foreground. The blend should make an idea
read in a moment, then reward a longer look with period atmosphere and
editorial detail.

The goal is not a generic stick-figure tutorial, an imitation of another
channel, or a substitute for historical evidence. It is a branded literature
format: deliberate illustrated worlds, legible characters, useful visual
metaphors, and local factual surfaces.

## The visual system

| Layer | Job | Rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Combat Woodblock world | Establish period, place, weather, and material mood. | Warm fibrous paper, carved ink contours, 3–5 flat colour fields, no generated factual text. The world stays optically stable. |
| Graphic Silhouette actor | Make a person, choice, emotion, or mechanism readable at phone size. | Filled 2D cutout masses, 8–12 px dark contour at 1080p, 1–3 colour regions, one unmistakable gesture. Not skeletal line figures. |
| Story signal | Turn a sentence into one clear visual action. | A prop, icon, route, page, door, card, or silhouette changes state. It must clarify the narration rather than decorate it. |
| Local editorial surface | State evidence, numbers, lists, questions, or a correction. | Built in Remotion/Manim after layout review. Attach to a real page, panel, scroll, or intentionally blank field; never float a generic gray box over the scene. |

### Palette and material

- Paper: `#E7D3A1`; carved ink: `#1B1A17`; deep indigo: `#17324D`.
- Rust `#B64F36` signals correction, impact, or danger; ochre `#D99B32`
  signals attention; teal `#2F7F7A` carries a route or relationship.
- Use intentional print grain, registration variation, and sparse hatching.
  Grain may add material life but cannot obscure a face, hand, object, or
  factual surface.
- Preserve real negative space. Do not fill a frame just because a field is
  empty.

## Narrative and edit grammar

Every meaningful sentence or contrast receives a distinct visual concept. A
list receives a visual beat for each meaningful item, but a repeated or
already-visible noun does not force a cut.

1. **Hook** — show a familiar problem, surprising object, or visual question.
2. **Contrast** — cut between a modern intuition and the historical or
   conceptual reality; use an intentional hard cut, page turn, route carry,
   or shape match.
3. **Correction** — replace the weak model with one clear action or physical
   metaphor. Prefer a door opening, path separating, paper folding, weight
   shifting, or group gathering to an explanatory paragraph.
4. **Mechanism** — make cause and effect visible with a silhouette, prop, map
   trace, or local diagram. Never draw a meaningless relationship graph.
5. **Reframe** — finish a chapter with the viewer’s changed question, not a
   random text card.

Cadence:

- Micro-events land every 1.5–3 seconds: a character gesture, prop reveal,
  smoke puff, route trace, cloud drift, page turn, or local overlay build.
- Major shots are normally 3–6 seconds. A composition may run to 8 seconds
  only when it has multiple timed reveals.
- Cut or materially change the scene at least every 6 seconds. Ten seconds is
  a block boundary, not permission for a ten-second static plate.
- Cut at anticipation, contact, reveal, recoil, result, or a narration
  emphasis—not at arbitrary frame positions.
- Reserve stillness for emphasis. World backgrounds do not constantly pan or
  wobble; motion belongs to characters, props, weather regions, and local
  information.

## Shot selection

Classify each beat before assets are assigned:

| Intent | Preferred surface | Example visual action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Academic or institutional claim | archive, document, hall, measured folio | finger traces a source line; a seal appears in an approved empty margin |
| Martial culture or physical idea | filled silhouette, gear, training threshold | one figure shifts a beam, opens a gate, or sets down a bag; no technical sequence |
| Journey or regional movement | scenic world plus deterministic map inset | river becomes route ribbon; map nodes appear locally |
| Myth correction | contrast plate, crossed false model, then replacement | a heavy false model cracks away; the useful model enters |
| List or taxonomy | repeated icon/card slots, matched framing | one item appears per narrated item, then resolves into the next shot |
| Emotional reframe | character/world and a single prop | the learner closes a book, steps through a door, or looks toward the next world |

Do not solve an abstract point with a blank book, a generic gray callout, or a
diagram whose nodes lack reviewed relationships. Remove an overlay if it does
not state information the picture cannot carry by itself.

## Motion discipline

1. Lock the world camera unless the shot plan names a small, eased push toward
   an explicit focal point.
2. Stage the foreground on a plausible plane: feet on deck, floor, threshold,
   or mat; hands near an object they are meant to touch. Never place a cutout
   over crates, faces, archival evidence, or the exit transition.
3. When a narrator says a decisive verb, time the visible action to it. For
   example, `vanish` triggers a short local ink-puff and a clean woosh, then
   removes the actor before the next archival cut-in.
4. Use matched directional or shape transitions: route to river, circle to
   seal, smoke to steam, page edge to paper field. Reject decorative tears,
   random wipes, and full-background shake.
5. Generated video is optional and only earns its cost when it adds coherent
   character, object, or localized environmental motion. Local editorial
   assembly remains canonical.

## Acceptance checklist

- [ ] A frame reads as the same channel without a title or citation.
- [ ] A foreground actor has a clear supporting plane, scale, and gesture.
- [ ] The woodblock world establishes mood without pretending to be archival
  evidence.
- [ ] Every overlay adds information and is attached to an intentional
  surface.
- [ ] At least one meaningful visual event lands within every 3 seconds and
  the cutaway rule is satisfied.
- [ ] No source identity, study asset, generated factual text, generic
  relationship graph, or skeletal technique tutorial reaches the output.
