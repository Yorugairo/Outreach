## Script v2 (~575 words, ~3:00)

**[s01 — cold open ON THE TILL PLATE, captions punching in word groups; till
sounds under]**

She earns half of what he does. She retires first. `[post-key]`

He's a banker in Manhattan, counting his bonus. She's a budtender, counting
the till in my dispensary — I own it, and I worked in risk at JPMorgan. The
difference isn't discipline and it isn't salary — it fits on a single
receipt, and by the end of this video you'll calculate yours.

**[s02 — the banker's apartment]**

The banker first. I've stood in this apartment — I know it before the door
opens. A suit rack that cost a paycheck. A watch box with a loan inside it.
A television wider than the couch, and a rowing machine holding up laundry.
Half the closet still has tags on. `[post-key]`

I used to risk-score portfolios for a bank. This room is a portfolio. Every
position is a feeling he paid to stop having.

**[s03 — coin stack, calendar]**

Now watch the account. Rent clears and the balance flinches. Bonus month is
a memory by March. One blown transmission and this whole apartment runs on
a credit card it can't pay off. Ninety-five grand a year — and no floor
under any of it. `[post-key]`

And he's not reckless. He's running the exact play he was handed: earn
more, upgrade, repeat. The play is the problem.

**[s04 — the budtender's apartment]**

The budtender makes about half that. Walk in and the first thing you notice
is floor. A couch that gets sat on. Exactly the kitchen tools that get
used. On the table, a paper ledger, and a jar her tips pass through on the
way to somewhere that pays *her*. Eighteen months of expenses, sitting
quietly. When you count other people's cash for eight hours a day, you stop
worshipping it.

**[s05 — trading desk vs register, bar vs shields]**

I've watched money from both sides of the counter — risk reports upstairs,
register downstairs. Run the two of them side by side. He out-earns her
almost two to one. She holds ten times his margin. `[pre-key]` Not because
she's smarter. Because of one relationship he never noticed he was in.

**[s06 — host, spec sheet]**

The relationship between stuff and money. These days I build things with AI
for a living, and that job brands one lesson into you: outcomes come from
systems, not intentions. Money is a product like any other — and this video
is its spec. The rules the budtender ran without ever writing them down.
Every one of them is the same three dials: risk, reward, and time. Rule one
is hiding on every receipt you've ever thrown away. It's still there at the
end of this video, and I'll show you how to calculate yours.

**[s07 — receipt, second line appears]**

Rule one: every price tag is a down payment. The number on the receipt is
what it costs to *get* the thing. Under it, invisible, is what it costs to
*keep* the thing — space, upkeep, attention. Nobody invoices you for the
second line. You pay it anyway.

**[s08 — rowing machine, rent pipe]**

Take the rowing machine. His place runs eighteen hundred a month for nine
hundred square feet. Two dollars a square foot, every month. The machine
and its clearance take about twenty square feet. Forty dollars a month.
Nearly five hundred a year in rent — paid to a laundry rack. `[post-key]`
That's one object, in one apartment. Now do the whole country.

**[s09 — storage map, callback]**

This country has more self-storage facilities than McDonald's `[verify]`.
Remember the banker's spare room? That's a storage unit with better carpet.
He pays rent on it every month. The invoice just never arrives.

**[s10 — host, direct]**

That's your second receipt — and it's the small one. Rule two is what the
budtender does with the gap, the money the banker never even sees. And it
isn't "spend less." `[pre-key]` Spending less is how you stay exactly where
you are — she'd tell you that herself. There's a retired engineer the
internet calls Mr. Money Mustache who proved what it actually is: one
number that moves your retirement date more than your salary ever will.
That's next.

## What deliberately did NOT change

- **The v5 hook** — it is the approved calibration reference (doc 30 §2);
  the persona was derived partly *from* it.
- Characters, arithmetic, entity seed (Mr. Money Mustache), the ring
  (receipt opens, receipt closes), all slot boundaries and asset needs.
- Runtime: ~560 → ~575 words. The pass adds lens, not length.

## The honest read

v1 was already voice-adjacent — it was written toward this operator before
the persona was formalized. The pass therefore changes the narrator's
**lens** more than the words: rooms become portfolios, failures become
structure, and the risk-analyst is *present in the prose* instead of only
in the bio line. The two biggest concrete wins are the black-screen catch
(a doctrine violation that survived v1's approval) and the pause marks
(previously doctrine, now actually in the script). Final judgment on both
versions belongs to synthesized audio, per the standing rule.
