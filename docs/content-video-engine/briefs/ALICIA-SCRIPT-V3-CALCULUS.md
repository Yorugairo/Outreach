## The three-way verdict

**Did the calculus make us meaningfully stronger? Yes — and the delta is
specifically structural, which is what the map was for.**

1. **v2 → v3 closes real holes, not cosmetic ones.** v2 was already the
   voice winner, but the ledger shows what it was missing: no head-fake, no
   midpoint reversal (rule one arrived as a *lecture beat* — s07 just
   starts explaining), no anaphora arc, no ring echo (it opens on the till
   and ends on a tease), and its payoff slid past the segment boundary.
   v3 fixes all five while keeping every approved element — hook,
   characters, arithmetic, entity seed, T12 dissent. The single biggest
   upgrade is the pivot: "spend less" is now *offered straight at 0:55 and
   demolished at 1:35*, which converts v2's explanation into a reversal
   the viewer feels they earned.

2. **v3 vs AOY-C: we now beat them on their own axis.** C's strength was
   never voice (zero first-person, as predicted) — it was retention
   mechanics and sourcing. The ledger shows C actually runs *thin* on the
   mechanics its own doctrine prescribes: no promise, no head-fake, no
   pivot, a mid-video CTA their own outro rules forbid. Its two genuine
   assets — verbatim attribution armor and the bonus-volatility insight —
   are one adopted as our hard gate and one already banked for rule two.
   What C still does better: primary-quote density. Our `[verify]` flag on
   the storage stat is the honest cost of not fabricating a citation.

3. **The honest caveats.** First: v3's machinery is invisible on the page
   to a casual reader — v2 and v3 read similarly; they should *time*
   differently. The claim that the head-fake/pivot/ring are worth their
   words is falsifiable only by ear and by retention data, and the
   judge-by-ear rule stands. Second: at 3:00 the duty roster runs near its
   density ceiling — one more system and the segment would have no air.
   That is itself a finding for the phase guides: **the roster scales down
   by dropping counts, never by dropping the pivot, the promise, or the
   ring.** Third: v3 was written by the author of the map; the real test
   of T2 is whether a fresh context produces this from the guides alone.

**Recommendation:** the method holds. Proceed to T2 — and carry the density
finding into every guide: at short runtimes, systems merge (A4 = pivot =
head-fake resolution is one construction, not three) rather than stack.
