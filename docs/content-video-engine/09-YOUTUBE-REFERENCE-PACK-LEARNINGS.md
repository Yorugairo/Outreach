## 1. Executive decisions

1. **Replace pose repetition with causal state changes.** The unit of visual storytelling is
   `state_from → action → state_to`, not a narration paragraph mapped to a static pose.
2. **Use an anchorable 2D cast for technique mechanics.** Attacker and defender keep persistent,
   opposing colors, depth order, and body ownership. Generic overlapping stick lines remain a
   legacy/non-grappling option only.
3. **Cover each technique with multiple shot functions.** Wide context, contact/grip cut-in,
   balance or hip-angle view, wrong/right contrast, force diagram, and held result each answer a
   different question.
4. **Cut on causality, not on a timer.** Anticipation, contact, recoil, transition, and result are
   natural edit points. Internal motion can carry a longer shot when the visual state keeps
   changing.
5. **Make transitions inherit the subject.** A body arc, force arrow, mat line, grip, map route,
   or diagram shape should motivate the hand-off to the next shot.
6. **Keep instructional mechanics deterministic and reviewed.** Image/video generation may help
   with licensed keyframe ideation or non-instructional atmosphere. It does not decide anatomy,
   limb placement, contact, or technique correctness.

## 3. What each reference family contributes

### 3.1 BJJ fight shorts: physical legibility

`BJJ vs Taekwondo`, `BJJ vs Aikido`, and `BJJ vs Muay Thai` provide the closest movement grammar:

- High-contrast character colors make intertwined bodies separable.
- Wide shots establish who owns each limb and where weight is supported.
- Close-ups isolate the decisive contact instead of asking one full-body pose to explain it.
- Motion reads as `anticipation → acceleration/redirection → contact → consequence → held result`.
- The camera moves closer at contact and resets only after the result becomes readable.
- Force types differ: linear impact, circular redirection, balance break, and ground control should
  not share one generic tween.

What **not** to copy: character designs, exact choreography, frames, effects, or branding. P13
adopts the causal grammar and identity contrast only.

### 3.2 Concept animation: make the world obey the lesson

`Animation vs. Geometry` demonstrates that a low cut rate is not visually static. Points, pivots,
lines, angles, and constraints are introduced only when they become causal. For P13:

- A frame arrow should become the line of resistance.
- A hip marker should become the fulcrum in the leverage diagram.
- A body arc can persist into a trajectory overlay or scene transition.
- Construction graphics should appear at the moment they explain a change, then recede.

This is the strongest model for biomechanics scenes: the diagram is not an inserted slide; it is
the same action with its hidden structure revealed.

### 3.3 Long-form explainers: stable visual grammar

The Historically, Animated History, Trust Me Bro, and Paint Explainer samples use maps, cards,
labels, photos, mascots, and limited character animation. Their reusable lesson is consistency:

- Reuse a small number of layouts so the viewer learns the interface.
- Change pose, prop, camera, label, or object state inside a scene before changing the whole scene.
- Let maps, headlines, artifacts, and diagrams carry factual state changes.
- Use recurring chapter motifs as resets.
- Increase visual density only when the subject becomes more complex.

For P13, this supports a repeatable technique template without making every video look identical:
the **shot functions** stay stable while the action states, diagrams, errors, and story motifs vary.

### 3.4 Action and music animation: timing hierarchy

`Combat gods II`, `The Ultimate Showdown`, `The Assault!!`, and `Avatar Element Animation 2`
demonstrate:

- Anticipation and recoil sell force before anatomical detail does.
- A simple environment becomes a choreography grid when bodies and props persist.
- Screen direction and architectural anchors prevent spatial resets.
- Each force benefits from a distinct motion curve, texture, color, and sound.
- Music-led edits can use the track as timing authority; narration-led instruction should not.

P13 should borrow impact hierarchy and persistence, not fight-spectacle density.

### 3.5 Martial-art catalog explainer: evidence plus shorthand

`Every Martial Art Type Explained in 12 Minutes` alternates a reusable heading/icon layout,
demonstration images, and simplified diagrams. For P13 this validates:

- one or two defining mechanics per section;
- photo/reference evidence and diagram shorthand serving different purposes;
- direct photo-to-diagram swaps;
- consistent comparison layouts for wrong/right or variation A/B.

Commercial use still requires rights-cleared source imagery. Corpus video references may guide
human review, but they do not automatically grant reusable frame rights.

## 4. P13 shot grammar

Every 30–60 second technique explainer should contain the following functions. A single rendered
shot may satisfy more than one function if the change remains readable.

| Function | Viewer question | Required visual evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `result_preview` | What are we building toward? | Brief readable finish/result, then rewind or reset |
| `wide_setup` | Who is where? | Full cast, stable screen direction, visible support/contact points |
| `contact_closeup` | What exactly connects? | One grip/frame/contact isolated; body ownership remains color-coded |
| `mechanic_transition` | What changes the position? | Continuous before/action/after state with an explicit path |
| `wrong_right_compare` | Why does the common attempt fail? | Same framing and start state; only the decisive variable changes |
| `force_diagram` | Why does it work? | Fulcrum/frame/wedge/force overlay attached to the action, not a detached slide |
| `result_hold` | What should I recognize? | Stable final state held long enough to inspect, with minimal labels |
| `story_or_persona_cutaway` | Why keep watching? | Short, non-instructional pattern interrupt tied to the script |

Minimum coverage:

- at least one `wide_setup`;
- at least two detail cut-ins using different focal targets;
- at least one continuous `state_from → action → state_to`;
- one wrong/right comparison when the source contains a verified common error;
- one force/leverage diagram when it materially explains the technique;
- one held result;
- no more than two consecutive shots with the same framing and visual function.

Existing cadence budgets remain: a meaningful visual change at least every 6 seconds in landscape
and every 3 seconds in vertical. The BJJ references justify faster 1.5–2.5 second shot changes
around physical exchanges, but that is an action-cluster preset, not a whole-video requirement.

## 5. Storyboard action contract

The current `BJJActionScene` direction is correct. Reference-derived recipes should compile into a
deterministic contract such as:

```json
{
  "recipe_version": "shot_recipe.v1",
  "function": "contact_closeup",
  "cast": {
    "attacker": "white_gi_blue_belt",
    "defender": "black_gi_purple_belt"
  },
  "state_from": "reviewed_start_state",
  "action": "reviewed_action_id",
  "state_to": "reviewed_end_state",
  "camera": {
    "framing": "grip_closeup",
    "move": "push_in",
    "focus": "reviewed_contact_id"
  },
  "motion": {
    "path": "linear|arc|pivot|compression|release",
    "phases": ["anticipation", "action", "contact", "recovery"]
  },
  "overlays": ["reviewed_force_or_contact_overlay"],
  "sound_cues": ["movement", "contact", "aftermath"],
  "reference_refs": ["rights_cleared_reference_id"]
}
```

Rules:

- `state_from`, `action`, `state_to`, focal targets, and overlays must resolve to reviewed assets.
- The first rendered frame must preserve the prior shot's cast positions unless the transition
  explicitly resets space.
- Each action phase must change silhouette, contact, center of mass, camera, or overlay state.
- `reference_refs` may be empty for deterministic library actions. If populated, each reference
  requires provenance and permission metadata.
- Generated pixels never become proof that a technique is correct.

## 6. Armbar v2 coverage plan

This is a coverage recipe, not a new technique claim. Final states and wording must resolve to the
approved corpus transcript and rights-cleared technique references.

1. **Result preview:** a readable finish silhouette; immediate rewind motif.
2. **Wide setup:** both practitioners fully visible in the verified starting position.
3. **Grip/contact cut-in:** isolate the first decisive control; preserve practitioner colors.
4. **Wrong/right comparison:** split or match-cut the verified common error against the correction.
5. **Angle transition:** return wide enough to show the body/hip relationship changing.
6. **Head/leg or secondary-control cut-in:** isolate the next verified control without hiding body
   ownership.
7. **Living leverage diagram:** freeze or slow the same action; reveal fulcrum, load, and effort on
   top of the bodies; then resume.
8. **Result hold and recognition checklist:** full state, two or three source-backed labels, then
   CTA.

The story follows the technique because each narration clause points to a new visual question.
Repeated full-body pose fades are not valid substitutes for steps 3–7.

## 7. Sound grammar

Narration remains the authority for instructional videos. Use three sparse effect layers:

1. **Movement:** cloth, mat shift, short whoosh, or diagram friction.
2. **Contact:** grip click, controlled impact, or force-marker transient.
3. **Aftermath:** floor/body settle, breath, release, or brief room tone.

Do not use one generic hit for every state. Linear impact, circular redirection, compression, and
release need different envelopes. Music stays optional for the first thin slice; silence and room
tone are preferable to a bed that masks explanation.

## 8. Gate A and automated QC additions

Gate A should reject a technique storyboard when any of the following is true:

- a narration step lacks a new state, action, diagram, or shot purpose;
- more than two consecutive shots repeat the same framing/function;
- the cast changes identity, color, side, or depth without an explicit transition;
- a close-up removes the context needed to identify body ownership;
- a technique action lacks a reviewed source or deterministic library definition;
- a force overlay contradicts the contact points or action path;
- the storyboard lacks wide setup, detail cut-ins, and a held result;
- a generative asset is asked to establish instructional truth.

Render QC should additionally compare frame/asset hashes and action IDs to flag repeated visuals,
verify action-phase duration is nonzero, and report per-scene visual-function diversity. These are
flags for review, not substitutes for a knowledgeable human technique check.

## 9. Tool boundary

| Tool class | Appropriate P13 use | Not appropriate |
|---|---|---|
| Manim / deterministic 2D vector | diagrams, overlays, camera, typography, reviewed action states | inventing anatomy from text |
| Pose-conditioned image generation | licensed keyframe ideation, background or story cut-in drafts | source of truth for intertwined limbs |
| Video generation (for example Kling-class tools) | short non-instructional atmosphere or transition experiments with rights-cleared inputs | technique mechanics, continuity-critical actions |
| Image generation (for example Midjourney-class tools) | style exploration and rights-cleared non-technical illustrations | consistent multi-shot cast or unreviewed grappling positions |
| Operator-owned/reference video | pose study, shot timing, manual action annotation | implicit model-training permission |

The next thin slice does not require a new paid visual provider. It requires a small reviewed
action/state library, shot-function coverage, and a renderer that moves continuously between
those states. Add generative providers only after that deterministic slice passes Gate B.
