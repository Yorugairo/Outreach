## 1. The attention ladder

Every number owns a different mechanism — they stack, they don't conflict:

| Window | Mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| 0–2s | Visual stun — colors, motion, brightness ("faceless channels live or die here") |
| 1–3s | Stop-the-scroll hook window — highest-leverage part of any script |
| 8s | "Is this the right video" decision — canonical decision window |
| 15s | Delay tolerance — greeting intros ("Hey guys, welcome back…") die here |
| 30s | Cliff diagnostic — 70%+ retention at 30s = hook working |
| 60s | The One Minute Wall |

**One Minute Wall structure:** 0–8s establish what the video is about ·
8–30s create a curiosity gap that demands resolution · 30–60s deliver a
mini-payoff AND open a bigger loop. If you lose 60%+ by minute one, the
problem is in this window — not later.

## 2. Hooks

- Thumbnail earns the click; the hook earns the view.
- One-sentence anatomy: **Target + Transformation + Stakes.** Their example:
  "If you're under 35 and don't understand how the next 18 months of
  inflation will hit your savings, you'll learn it the expensive way — let me
  show you the math instead."
- Hooks tease, never spoil: show the *what*, not the *how*.
- Specific numbers over vague amounts — a concrete number sparks an immediate
  question.
- The conversion test: does the hook create a question that *demands* an
  answer, or could the viewer move on comfortably?
- Signature-formula option (Agent Floppy style): same hook structure in every
  video ("From [shocking] to [shocking] to [shocking]. This is how every
  [subject]…") until it becomes a recognizable signature.

## 3. Retention architecture (the middle)

**4 Elements of Progression — all four run simultaneously; miss one and the
retention graph shows exactly where:**

1. New information every 15–30s — a fact, twist, or angle the viewer didn't have.
2. Progression toward a goal — each scene moves closer to the payoff.
3. Regression / obstacles — things going WRONG engages more than things going right.
4. Emotional change — flat emotion habituates; the register must shift.

Before writing each paragraph, ask which element it delivers. If none — rewrite.

**STR loops** (Setup → Tension → Resolution), nested at two scales: micro
loops closing every 30–60s; **4–6 macro loops per 15-minute video**, each
closing on a partial answer that opens the next bigger question. Never close
a loop without opening the next — new setup IMMEDIATELY.

**Rehook system — positional, not periodic:** rehooks at **30s, 1min, 3min,
and mid-video** (the spots where analytics consistently show drops). A rehook
re-justifies the next 60 seconds. Five templates: "But here's where it gets
weird…" · "What nobody knew at the time was…" · "This is where most people
get it wrong…" · "Let's fast-forward to…" · "But the real question is…"
(Rehooks *every 60–90s* is the remedy for a diagnosed Slow Bleed, not the default.)

**But/Therefore Rule:** every beat-to-beat connector is BUT or THEREFORE,
never AND-THEN. And-then = no causation, no tension.

**Point ordering:** open with a strong example but *not* the best; put the
best in the middle where retention peaks; close with third-best. Leading with
the best leaves nowhere to go.

**Breathing Room Principle:** intensity for 10 straight minutes habituates
the viewer — scripts that pile on from minute 1 to 12 underperform scripts
with intentional dips. The quiet verse makes the chorus hit.

**Runtime:** match length to the story, never pad — padding "gets felt within
60 seconds and tanks retention." Length tiers: T1 8–10 min · T2 12–18 · T3 20+.

## 4. Script quality control (three passes on one draft)

1. **Red Tape Theory — four checkpoints:** Connecting Thread (one through-line
   statable in a single sentence) · Easy to Follow · Deep Identification ·
   Perspective Change. Camera test: every line must create a filmable image —
   "showing with words"; a line that cannot be shown weakens the video
   (animatability audit: underline provable-on-screen, strike the rest).
2. **Never Repeat Rule / Highlighter Method:** highlight every concept; cut or
   merge anything highlighted twice. Deliberate callbacks stay; unconscious
   repetition is filler.
3. **"Would I Watch This?" test:** read the opening 50 words and the mid-video
   rehook out loud.

**Anti-AI Slop audit** *(partial — 3 of 8 patterns retrieved)*: short-period
fragments ("No jargon. No fluff. No excuses.") → rewrite with commas and
varied rhythm · colon abuse → max 2–3 per script · the "Most" opener ("Most
people believe…") → never; use a hot take or story.

## 5. Faceless personality (#49)

The channel has no face, so personality comes from the words. Three mechanisms:

1. **Commentary / reaction lines** — "Absolute shocker, right?" / "Yeah, you
   read that right." / "Let that sink in for a second." A recurring aside
   voice the viewer remembers.
2. **Rhetorical questions** — "But here's the question — how did he get away
   with it?" / "Sound crazy? It gets worse."
3. **Dark humor when appropriate** — setup-then-snap reversal ("…died of old
   age in 1955… just kidding. He died from a rattlesnake bite.").

Texture: direct "you" · "Imagine this:" for scene-setting · short punchy
sentences varied against longer breathing ones.

## 6. Content types (#58)

Write differently per type — same approach on two types underperforms on both:

- **Narrative** (crime, history, biography, mystery): runs on tension +
  resolution; STR loops dominant.
- **Educational** (finance, tech, science explainer): runs on curiosity +
  insight — open with a question the viewer can't answer themselves, pay it
  off with a specific mechanism, not a generic wrap.

Three non-negotiables before writing: know your viewer (4-part avatar —
demographics incl. phone-vs-TV, psychographics: beliefs, fears, fantasies) ·
do real research · build structure that earns the next 10 seconds.

## 7. Voiceover

- Voice selection is unopinionated: pick any voice you find good — it becomes
  the channel's personality. **Generic AI voice fine; real-person voice
  cloning banned** (inauthentic-content enforcement).
- **Write for the edit:** paragraphs 2–3 lines max, because on screen the
  script becomes VO with cuts every 2–4 seconds.
- Two-gear pacing: short sentences = tension, longer = context — deliberately.
- No AI-voice parameter guidance exists in the corpus (no ElevenLabs
  settings). Voice configuration is the operator's own problem.

## 8. Titles and thumbnails

- Never put a word before a number: "Top 20", not "The Top 20".
- Specificity beats sensationalism; promise only what the video delivers —
  mismatch clickbait is machine-detected and penalized.
- Post-upload iteration is doctrine: change title/thumbnail on
  underperformers via YouTube's native **Test & Compare**, not gut swaps.
- Top-performing formulas on record: regret frames ("X REGRETS…", "…and Now I
  Regret It") and exodus frames ("Why is EVERYONE Leaving X?").
- 150-pattern template library, browsable unauthenticated:
  https://ai.theartofyt.com/static/format_patterns.html
- Channel naming: 2-word brandable name; check YouTube for collisions.
- Faces in thumbnails on a faceless channel: no — kills sellability and delegation.

## 9. Diagnostics — retention graph shapes and benchmarks

| Shape | Meaning | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cliff (0–30s drop) | hook failed / thumbnail over-promised | rewrite the hook |
| Slow Bleed (gradual) | no retention mechanics | rehooks every 60–90s, BUT/THEREFORE, vary rhythm |
| Mid-Video Cliff | that section is boring/confusing/off-topic | cut or rewrite; mini-hook before it |
| Re-engagement Spike | viewers skipped filler | move the good part earlier, cut the weak section |
| Plateau after initial drop | excellent — right audience, content held | scale this format |

**Benchmarks:** 70%+ at 30s = hook working · 50%+ AVD ≈ top ~16% of YouTube ·
educational ~42% avg · documentary 35–45% · true crime top performers 65%+.
