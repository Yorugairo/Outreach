A callout ring has one use: a number or a point on a CHART (a datum target, or a stamp whose label is the number - the map's 25%). Never a ring on a picture (a plate, a still, a character). The focus on a picture's subject is the spotlight species
(`idle: "live"` so the held light breathes) - or a shimmer once it exists (docs_find 0 hits for shimmer/glint/sheen).

**Why:** twice in two days the operator caught circles: E49 §3 ("you're circling beneath to avoid circling") and then
the ring on the fab card's wafer - "drawing the ring on the wafer is dumb ... give it a light shimmer or a spotlight.
We have to stop drawing rings or circles on everything - that's just a cheap call-out mechanism, not a high quality
animation." And: "make sure all of the new additions pass the life check with the pixels shifting."

**How to apply:** (1) a species that only circles is refused at proposal time; the light is the default focus. (2) Every
new addition is measured for life on ITS OWN region over its hold - sample every 0.25 s, hash the region, no two
consecutive samples identical - before it is declared (the whole-frame M18 is not enough: a still thing on a breathing
page passes M18). (3) The crossings map's `25%` stamps are numbers - inside the rule; the compiler refuses a callout that targets a non-datum with no digit in its label. See [nothing-ever-goes-truly-still](nothing-ever-goes-truly-still.md), [chart-form-rulings-e50-e53](chart-form-rulings-e50-e53.md).
