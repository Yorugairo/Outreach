The first YouTube-search breakdown we have, on the first long-form video. **YouTube search overall: 19 views, 10:42
average view duration, 79.7 % average percentage viewed** - a strong number. Inside it, two named queries pull in
opposite directions:

- **"ai bubble"** - 5 views (26 % of search), **1:15**, **9.4 % viewed**. A broad topical query. Consistent bounce
  across five people, so this is the statistically meaningful row, and it is the NEGATIVE one.
- **"bravos research"** - 1 view, **12:56**, **96.2 % viewed**, 13.4 % of all search watch time from one person. An
  intent query: someone looking for this specific kind of analysis, who then watched essentially all of it.

**Why:** the operator, 2026-09-07: *"the youtube video got minimal traction, and had a high bounce rate. But the one
person so far who found the video from searching for Bravos Research, who was our target audience for the video,
watched for 13 minutes."* The blended bounce rate was hiding two different audiences. One of them is ours.

**How to apply.** Judge a video on the **intent cohort**, not the blended retention number, and do not let a topical
query's bounce read as "the video is weak" - it reads as "the package answered a question that viewer was not asking"
(E27/E24: sentence 1 answers the thumbnail; a broad-query viewer arrives with a different thumbnail in their head).
Two live consequences: competitor- and adjacent-channel queries are a real acquisition channel worth ranking for, and a
title/thumbnail aimed at a huge generic query buys views that leave at 9 %.

**Why this table is trustworthy** (asked 2026-09-07): not because of any view-counting nuance - it is filtered to
`Traffic source > YouTube search`, so every row is someone who typed a query and chose our result out of a page of
results. Intent by construction; feed hovers and autoplay sit under Browse/Suggested and cannot appear here. The
count-on-play change was **Shorts** (early 2025, with "engaged views" kept alongside); long-form still needs
intentional playback of meaningful length (~30 s is the widely reported figure, never officially published). So the
"ai bubble" row is not phantom traffic: five people cleared the threshold and averaged 1:15. They searched, chose us,
sampled the opening for a minute, and left - which is a real fault in the package, not an artifact of the metric.

**The caveats, so this is not over-read:** the intent row is **n = 1** - directional, not established; the topical row
at n = 5 carries more weight and is the one to act on first. Only 6 of the 19 search views are itemised here, and the
other 13 are what actually carry the 79.7 % - **pulling those remaining query terms is the cheapest next measurement we
have**, and it either confirms the split or breaks it. Related: [package-first-e27](package-first-e27.md), [opening-minute-e24](opening-minute-e24.md),
[youtube-retention-clock](youtube-retention-clock.md), [viewer-perception-test](viewer-perception-test.md), [thresholds-from-the-reference](thresholds-from-the-reference.md).
