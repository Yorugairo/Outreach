The operator on our `solo` sheet beside Bravos JPN 05:20/05:23.5: Bravos's primary lines carry "higher vibrancy/contrast/electricity" and its muted lines fade to a higher contrast; "we still need more electricity/glow to our lines, not sure what the right term is." The term is BLOOM: an emissive line = a near-white hot core + the saturated ink + a soft wide halo of that ink on the charcoal.

**Why:** E67 (inks are electric) was delivered as palette hexes and a faint halo (R26-228 `lpBloom`), not as an emissive line; I first read the sheet as "one dim number" (0.45 vs 0.3) and missed that BOTH ends differ.

**How to apply:** every primary series blooms by default; context/muted lines never do; a solo lifts the lit line and fades the rest harder and thinner. Numbers come from `measure_line_bloom.py` run on Bravos first ([thresholds-from-the-reference](thresholds-from-the-reference.md)), then ours; phone 390 px read. Carrier P69 T37b (takes T76's white-hot core forward). When comparing to Bravos, read BOTH the lit and the context ends, not one dial. Related: [bravos-reference](bravos-reference.md), [chart-reads-at-a-glance-e28](chart-reads-at-a-glance-e28.md).
