A ledger page fills its charcoal three ways: `page.field = "scribble"` (nib strokes, the original idea), `"soak"`
(E22's K-M seeps, the engine default) and the two-plate cross-fade (`page.plate` + `page.field_plate` + `page.board`,
the generated cream plate with the charcoal plate faded over it - doc 41's "the deckle is the feature"). I had read
E99 s18's "no scribble clearing to cream" as "keep the scribbles on the leave" and briefed P61 T4b on it.

**Why:** the operator, 2026-09-15: *"why are we keeping scribbles? i thought we said the scribbles should be a
back-up function"*; then, shown all three rendered from one source: *"the leave-soak is much better than the two
plate leave, but my wife and i are split on the cross-fade. The cross-fade is smooth, the soak gives the eye more
to watch. I think they should both be first class effects. When we are trying to maintain continuity, connecting
ideas, speaking across plates i think the cross-fade is the answer, when we are building an idea or introducing a
new idea or looking to fill space to separate ideas, the soak is the transition."*

**How to apply:** author the field by the sentence's JOB, never by the page's form: cross-fade = continuity (the
next plate carries the idea on), soak = a new idea or a separator. The leave is the soak's recede for both (the
plate fade-out is retired as a leave). Scribble is opt-in only; nothing is built for it. Before proposing a page
treatment, render the candidates from one golden source with only the field swapped and put the clips in front of
the operator - a still cannot show a field entry, and "these all need to be decisions that exist and are able to be
rendered." Recorded as E99 s35; s18's Apply line corrected. See [ledger-page-signature](ledger-page-signature.md), [soak-ink-is-a-plate](soak-ink-is-a-plate.md).
