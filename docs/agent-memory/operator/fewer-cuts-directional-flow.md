because we've been working on building the melts, the life, the rescale, recast, morph, transforms, the balls, inks
etc. the whole point of creating them was to improve our ability to live with less cuts and to be able to keep a
directional flow. that's why the camera work, choreography etc is so important that we built also."*

**Why:** I ranked the vocabulary by how many shipped cuts carry each word - and nothing has shipped since the
mechanisms were built, so the count ranked the old habits (dip, cut, hold) above the purpose of the new work, and
filed rescale/recast/morph/object-becomes-chart as "the regression" and "never shipped". They are the point: each
replaces a cut with a continuous move ("the balls" = the melt's ink balling up and leaving, E88).

**How to apply:** the compiler's objective is fewer cuts and a directional flow. A cut or a dip is the last resort
between two worlds, taken only after a transform (melt-then-splash, morph, recast, rescale, object-becomes-chart,
the door, the snap, a camera move) was refused by name for that pair, and every cut/dip row's `why` names the
transform it could not use. Carry-forward order: transforms + continuity moves first (with life and inks as page
defaults), then the arrivals, then the universal set. Owed: a flow read beside M46 (BACKLOG R26-189). Never present
a survival matrix as a ranking again. See [rebuild-is-not-a-base](rebuild-is-not-a-base.md), [frames-inward-not-tokens-outward](frames-inward-not-tokens-outward.md),
