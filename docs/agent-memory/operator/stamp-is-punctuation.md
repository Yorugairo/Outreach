The operator (2026-09-24), on stamps dropped on the same instant as the data point: "the stamp lands just after whatever pertinent motion, data point, or word/topic is introduced ... if we just drop the stamp at the exact same time as the data point, it's all one thing and we're asking them to process both at once, while also costing our own extended motion time ... I think of the stamp as the punctuation on the thing, so it has to follow immediately after not during."

**Why:** the stamp's appearance makes sense only once its referent has landed; following it reads as extended thought and buys motion time. A stamp on the same instant competes with the data point.

**How to apply:** time every stamp to the END of its word, or the end of the idea the sentence carries (author's pick), never mid-word or on a data mark's instant (E99 s112; carriers T81 engine anchor + advice, T82 re-time every H stamp). Related (s113): text on a plate or prop is allowed when intentional, narrative-driven and verified; the old no-text convention is gone, so garbled generator text is still refused on the frame read. Related: [engine-advises-author-decides](engine-advises-author-decides.md), [motion-moves-the-story](motion-moves-the-story.md).
