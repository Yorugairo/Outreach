## Reported positioning and output

- Approximately 90K subscribers, 9.7M total views and 160 videos.
- Publishing cadence: 6.6 long-form videos per week; reported 100% long-form and zero Shorts in the 90-day window.

- Recurring packaging: relatable money puzzle + “The Real Math” framing + mild urgency/alarm (“Don't Wait”, “Do This Or Lose It All”).
- Consultant's positioning read: how ordinary people afford apparently impossible purchases; the emotional question is whether everyone else is richer than the viewer.

## Suggested opportunities from the supplied analysis

1. Test Shorts adapted from concrete affordability puzzles. The consultant calls the missing Shorts layer the highest-leverage opportunity; this is a hypothesis, not demonstrated long-form conversion or incremental growth.
2. Extend the affordability series into tuition, groceries, weddings and childcare. Preserve a specific object/cost and recognizable household constraint.
3. Balance news-triggered spikes with evergreen affordability stories.
4. Test concrete consequence-led packaging for conceptual explainers to improve the median upload, not only chase outliers. Replacing conceptual topics is not adopted as an automatic rule.

## Implications for Money Physics

- Borrow the entry point: a visible purchase, bill or refinancing decision that creates a genuine puzzle for an ordinary person.
- Keep our differentiation: reveal the institutional mechanism and show source-bound math that explains who bears the cost. Concrete packaging should lead into depth, not displace it.
- Carry the person/prop through the explanation and back into the consequence. Use rich narrative worlds with selective evidence docks; full-ledger space is earned when comparison needs it.
- Separate packaging performance from visual cadence. This supplied analysis does not measure shot lengths. Our direct review of the Fed reference found at least 15 illustrated compositions plus a title in the opening minute using two-second samples; it did not establish a whole-video median.
- Current art coverage floor remains six distinct narrative/art candidates per minute (18 per three-minute pilot), not six compulsory cuts or a limit on internal visual development.
- Shorts research and a new affordability concept were offered in the pasted material, but are not commissioned by this note. No new work order, publication or episode rewrite is authorized here.
