## Taxonomy and visual grammar (initial pass)

**TYPES — observed chart/graphic species.** Long-history single line (USD/JPY); overlaid dual-series time line (USD/JPY + S&P 500); historical-event comparison with year capsules, vertical guides and decline badges; multi-series treasury holdings line; dual-unit holdings/yield overlay; axis-free market-line motif; bank/carry-trade flow schematic; map/node flow; highlighted document evidence. Those are distinct species: the pictorial flows and source-page overlay should not be counted as charts.

**ACTIONS — observed transformations.** Add series to a held plot; progressively mark selected years; land endpoint/historical glow dots, guides and compact percentage pills; mute comparison series to isolate Japan; retain a small context diagram while the line holds; project a dotted continuation; zoom/crop to the endpoint; add a terminal amount badge; overlay a second scale/series and endpoint X marks; reduce a chart into a contextless line-and-marker motif. Exact chart exits often fall between broad samples.

**TAXONOMY — encoding/layout/color.** Time is horizontal; yen, equity-index, holdings and yield are continuous line encodings. The 01:10 comparison explicitly assigns S&P 500 points to LHS and USD/JPY to RHS; the 06:38 chart assigns holdings to left-side billions and yield to right-side percent. Text and marker color reinforce series identity (dominant neon magenta vs white/gray); historical events use colored year chips and local labels rather than a separate legend alone. Dark slate panels and pale grid/axes are bounded by negative space; charts frequently share the frame with a right-hand explanatory sidecar. The twin scales are visibly named, but their overlaid slopes still risk implying a more direct relationship than the axes warrant; use aligned panels in our work unless the dual-axis reading is essential and reviewed. Inference: pink likely means narrative focus/yen/Japan, not a universal asset-category color—verify before transfer.

## EFFECTS / STYLE — motion grammar and VO relationship

- **Enter.** The opening yen chart is already built at 00:20, and the 01:35 crash stack is already populated at the focused window's first frame. Exact entry/exit choreography is therefore not claimed. In sampled chart action, the new S&P line draws across the held yen plot; at 06:38 the late-series endpoint accents are partly revealed before the complete holdings/yield curves are fully bright.
- **Transform.** The composition tends to retain the original chart and add a layer: a second series, event-year guides, markers, short badges, a dotted scenario path, or a right-side schematic. In the Treasury chart, China/UK lose salience instead of disappearing from the story; Japan becomes the focus. This is a same-data state change rather than a fresh chart cut.
- **Camera.** The clearest observed camera move is 05:44–05:45: the Treasury history is cropped/zoomed to its latest segment before the “$100 Billion” badge lands. Context dims but remains partly visible; at the crop edge, the historical x-axis is no longer the primary reading surface. Useful as a reveal, risky if the zoom severs scale/source context.
- **Annotations.** The recurring sequence is line/endpoint → localized point or year pin → concise label/badge. Guides connect event to position; the 2007 capsule and later 1987/2001 labels form a reusable event key. White curve leaders and endpoint glows keep attention at the current datum. The right-hand BUY/SELL mechanism runs in parallel with the historical chart, rather than claiming that its illustrative tokens are plotted values.
- **Hold/exit.** Several charts remain on screen across many seconds while captions advance, acting as explanatory canvases. Grid sampling cannot locate exact exits for most of them; do not infer a smooth morph where the transition could be a cut. Later the detailed comparison is deliberately reduced to a sparse line/marker motif, but the exact state-by-state removal order falls between 25-second samples.
- **Audio / VO sync.** Timed captions give strong narrative alignment for the 2007 comparison (about 01:06–01:21), the $100bn reveal (captioned 05:43–05:48; badge visible by 05:45), and the yield claim (captioned 06:21–06:27; dedicated dual-unit chart observed at 06:38). This suggests emphasis-by-reveal, not uniform syllable-by-syllable syncing. Assessment uses caption timestamps, not a waveform or isolated SFX listen; no specific sound-effect claim is made.
- **Style.** Charcoal chart panels, pale grid/labels, vivid magenta emphasis, white secondary series, tight rectangular badges, thin leaders and small pictograms create a consistent “analyst desk” world. Titles and date/unit labels do the heavy lifting; tiny source/date footers are visible in the 1280×720 frames but low-salience. The later dual-axis overlay is an observed design choice, not an automatic recommendation: two differently scaled lines can invite false slope/correlation reading unless axes, units, color mapping and the comparison's limits stay explicit. Do not copy Bravos' logo, mascot, exact palette or chart art.

## Financial argument and reveal order

**Observed presentation order:** (1) recent yen intervention as the hook; (2) the 2007 yen/S&P overlay, expanded into a 1987/2001/2007 historical pattern; (3) carry-trade bank/fund flow; (4) an attributed $14.2T estimate; (5) a conditional investor-sell path; (6) Japan's Treasury holdings and a terminal $100bn badge; (7) Treasury holdings against the 30-year yield; (8) US/Japan spillover and systemic-risk framing; (9) a narrower risk qualification; (10) channel CTA.

**Inference about the rhetoric:** it steps from a current event to analogy, then mechanism and scale, then a transmission scenario, then a second piece of exposure evidence, before the counterweight/qualification. This reveal order makes a chain feel coherent but does not itself prove the links. In particular, historical co-movement, illustrative BUY/SELL icons, the “some estimates” capital figure, the narrated Treasury-sale number and the yield comparison are all presented claims/scenarios here; none was independently fact-checked for this visual report. Keep observation, source-attributed assertion and scenario visibly distinct in any adaptation.
