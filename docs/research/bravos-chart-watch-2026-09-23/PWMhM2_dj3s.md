## Early taxonomy and design inventory

- **Chart species observed:** single-series time line (Japan and Argentina yields); dual-axis comparison (US/Japan yields; debt/GDP/inflation; debt/GDP/deficit; debt/GDP/USD purchasing power); historical line with colored time-window/segment emphasis and point/event annotations; line chart with recession-band backdrop; filled area plus smooth reference curve for the closing strategy promotion. Not every graphic is a chart: maps, node-link/flow diagrams, precedent cards, cycle schematic, and isolated policy-choice icons are distinct scene forms.
- **Encodings/layout:** time is consistently horizontal; quantitative values are vertical, with unit/% ticks and date/source footers. Dual-axis charts place separate measures on left and right scales and use legends. Emphasis is selective color on a common series, point rings/year pills, glows, dashed ellipses, or contextual vertical bands. Charts are centered with large margins; explanatory icons occupy side rails or cards below/right of the plot.
- **Palette/typography:** nearly black/navy canvas and charcoal plot; rose/magenta titles and annotations; white text/lines; series colors include yellow (Japan yields), white (US yields), rose (debt/inflation), blue/cyan (deficit or purchasing power), green (cycle/flow or promo area), gray for de-emphasized series and recession bands. Bright highlights compete for attention, so legends, axis labels and source/date footer carry essential semantic weight.

## TYPES — chart and visual species

Observed as data charts: (1) single-series time lines for yields; (2) same-period multi-series comparisons, including US/Japan yields; (3) long-horizon debt/GDP lines with highlighted sub-periods; (4) dual-measure overlays such as debt/GDP with inflation, deficit, or USD purchasing power; (5) a market-index line with vertical recession bands; and (6) a filled area beside a pale reference curve in the closing Bravos strategy promotion. The debt-cycle sine-like curve is best classified as a schematic, not a measured series: no source, unit, or dated x-axis was legible in the inspected state. Maps, mechanism diagrams, precedent cards, and policy-choice icons are separate graphic species, not charts.

Encoding/layout observed: time mostly runs left-to-right, rates and ratios use labeled percentage axes, and a legend differentiates concurrent series. Where two measures share a chart, left/right axis labels and legend colors carry the unit mapping; this makes the pairing quick to scan but also raises a comparison risk if viewers read visual height as directly comparable. The chart is usually the center panel; a mechanism, icon group, or explanatory card uses a side rail or the space below/right of it. The chart layer is sometimes dimmed or softened when an explanatory diagram takes priority.

Color is semantic and selective rather than a universal legend: yellow for the Japan-yield line; white for US yield and some base traces; rose/magenta for debt/inflation emphasis, event labels, and alert marks; cyan/blue for deficit or dollar-purchasing-power series; green for a selected cycle segment, guide, flow tokens, or the promotional filled area; gray for background/history/recession context. Bright rings, pills, glows, dashed guides, and oval callouts form a second annotation layer. The nearly black/navy ground and white/gray axes keep those accents high-contrast; pink headings and compact subtitles establish chart identity. Source/date footers are present on inspected chart pages, though their small text was not transcribed or audited.
