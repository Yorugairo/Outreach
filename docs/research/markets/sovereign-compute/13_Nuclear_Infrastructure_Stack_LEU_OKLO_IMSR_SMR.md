### **Moat and Technology: The Physics of the Aurora Powerhouse**

The Aurora Powerhouse architecture systematically dismantles the legacy capital friction associated with traditional nuclear plants. Operating at a modular scale, recently uprated to support variants from 15 MWe up to a maximum electrical output of 75 MWe, the design relies on a liquid-metal (sodium) cooling system combined with metallic HALEU fuel enriched up to 19.75% U-235.

The structural cost-efficiencies of this fast-spectrum physics design compared to high-pressure light-water configurations are profound. Traditional light-water reactors require massive, highly pressurized containment domes and active pumping mechanisms to circulate water. This generates immense capital costs, rigid supply chain vulnerabilities, and extensive construction timelines. In stark contrast, the Aurora's liquid sodium coolant operates in a pool-type configuration at near-ambient pressure. Sodium possesses a significantly higher boiling point and superior thermal conductivity compared to water, allowing the reactor core to transfer heat efficiently without relying on pressurized systems.

Furthermore, the fast-neutron spectrum physics inherently provide a negative temperature coefficient of reactivity. If the core temperature rises due to a loss of external power or pump failure, the metallic fuel naturally expands, reducing nuclear reactivity, while the liquid sodium automatically continues to carry heat away via gravity-driven natural circulation. Even the decay heat post-shutdown is managed passively; air is pulled in naturally over the outside of the vessel, removing heat via convection without the need for electrically powered fans. This passive, self-stabilizing safety profile eliminates the need for complex, active emergency core cooling systems, shrinking the required physical footprint and allowing for true factory-scale module fabrication. This design heritage is not theoretical; it is fully validated by the Experimental Breeder Reactor-II (EBR-II), a fast reactor that safely operated at the Idaho National Laboratory (INL) from 1964 to 1994, proving it could shut down and cool itself using only the laws of physics. The power conversion is handled via a supercritical CO2 Brayton cycle, driving overall plant efficiency above 40%.

## **IV. Terrestrial Energy ($IMSR): High-Temperature Industrial Heat and Duration Risk**

Terrestrial Energy directly targets this sector via its Integral Molten Salt Reactor (IMSR) architecture, shifting the paradigm away from pure electricity toward dual-use high-temperature industrial heat.

### **Moat and Technology: The Standard Assay Bypass**

The IMSR operates under a fundamentally different physical model than both Oklo's sodium fast-reactor and traditional light-water platforms. The IMSR400 configuration generates an overall power plant potential of 390 MWe through twin reactors. The profound technological moat of the IMSR lies in its utilization of a liquid molten salt mixture that serves simultaneously as both the nuclear fuel and the primary coolant. Unlike solid fuel rods that degrade, swell, and require complex mechanical shuffling, the liquid fuel salt allows for homogeneous burnup and the chemical containment of fission products directly within the fluid. Heat dissipation is achieved safely through the convective flow of the fuel salt.

Crucially, the IMSR completely bypasses the immediate supply chain chokepoint currently gripping the advanced nuclear sector: it does not require HALEU. The reactor is specifically engineered to operate on Standard Assay Low-Enriched Uranium (LEU enriched to under 5%), which is globally abundant and processed through established, legacy supply chains. This engineering choice immediately neutralizes the fuel supply dependencies that plague fast-reactor companies reliant on domestic HALEU buildouts, removing significant upstream procurement friction. The design integrates the primary reactor components, including the graphite moderator, into a sealed and replaceable reactor core unit with an operating lifetime of seven years, simplifying operational maintenance.

### **The Addressable Market: Margin Expansion through Thermal Energy**

Terrestrial Energy's structural advantage is its ability to supply heat at exceptionally high temperatures, a requirement for massive industrial applications such as petrochemical refining, hydrogen generation, and chemical synthesis. Traditional light-water SMRs cannot achieve the thermal output required for these heavy industrial processes.

By outputting direct high-temperature industrial heat alongside electricity, the IMSR drastically alters the margin profile of the nuclear utility model. The company can secure long-term, high-margin thermal delivery contracts with chemical conglomerates, while simultaneously utilizing secondary heat exchangers to generate electricity for grid or data center operations. ## **V. NuScale Power ($SMR): The Water-Cooled Fleet Standard and the Heavy Forging Bottleneck**

NuScale Power anchors the traditional, evolutionary approach to small modular reactors. While Oklo and Terrestrial experiment with exotic liquid metals and molten salts, NuScale leverages the deeply established physics of pressurized light-water reactors, miniaturizing them into the factory-fabricated NuScale Power Module (NPM).

### **Moat and Regulatory Monopoly: The Standard Design Approval**

NuScale’s absolute structural moat is not rooted in groundbreaking fast-fission physics, but in a regulatory monopoly. NuScale is currently the first and only SMR technology company to possess a fully certified design from the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission. The NRC initially certified a 50 MWe module in 2023, but more crucially, in May 2025, the NRC completed a 22-month review to approve the uprated 250 MWt (77 MWe) version of the NPM via a Standard Design Approval (SDA).

This SDA constitutes an immense, virtually insurmountable barrier to entry for early-stage competitors. It indicates that the fundamental safety case and passive safety features have survived the most rigorous, multi-year regulatory audit on the planet. Any client seeking to deploy NuScale technology faces drastically reduced site licensing friction, as the core technology is already federally validated. The modules are designed to be scaled in flexible arrays, with the flagship VOYGR-12 configuration capable of accommodating up to 12 modules for a total gross output of 924 MWe.

### **Commercial Integration and the Doosan Forging Bottleneck**

The commercialization pipeline is heavily insulated through NuScale’s exclusive global strategic partnership with ENTRA1 Energy, an independent American energy production platform. Under this framework, ENTRA1 finances, owns, and operates the generating assets, utilizing NuScale modules inside the facilities. This partnership recently yielded a landmark collaborative agreement with the Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA) to deploy up to 6 GW of NuScale capacity across the TVA’s seven-state service region, the largest SMR deployment program in U.S. history.

However, a structural bottleneck audit reveals a severe vulnerability. The capital-intensive nature of pressurized water reactors creates a critical supply chain chokepoint: ultra-heavy steel forging. Because the NuScale system utilizes highly pressurized water, the containment vessels must be manufactured to extraordinary metallurgical tolerances using massive, nuclear-grade steel forgings. Globally, only a fraction of heavy industrial facilities possess the necessary materials certifications, precision forging capabilities, and nuclear-grade quality systems required for this level of manufacturing.

NuScale is entirely dependent on Doosan Enerbility in South Korea to execute this manufacturing. Doosan's finite factory floor space acts as a strict cap on NuScale's global deployment velocity.
