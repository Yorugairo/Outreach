# Track 3: Vertex & Polygon Decimation Algorithms, QEM & Retopology Workflows

## 1. Executive Summary & Optimization Taxonomy

Geometric simplification balances two competing constraints: reducing vertex count to satisfy hardware fill-rate, vertex transformation, and memory bandwidth budgets, while preserving surface curvature, silhouette fidelity, UV coordinates, and deformation eligibility. In digital content creation (DCC) and real-time asset pipelines, mesh reduction is governed by three primary methodologies:
1. **Quadric Error Metric (QEM) Decimation:** Pair contraction $(v_1, v_2) \to \bar{v}$ guided by fundamental error quadrics (Garland & Heckbert, 1997).
2. **Topological Inversion (Un-Subdivide):** Systematic decimation of regular quad meshes generated by Catmull-Clark or simple subdivision.
3. **Field-Aligned Quadrangulation / Remeshing:** Rebuilding the discrete manifold along principal curvature directions (Instant Meshes, Quad Remesher / Mixed-Integer Quadrangulation).

```
                            GEOMETRIC SIMPLIFICATION & RETOPOLOGY TAXONOMY
                                                  |
           +--------------------------------------+--------------------------------------+
           |                                      |                                      |
           v                                      v                                      v
  TRIANGLE EDGE-COLLAPSE (QEM)            QUAD GRID INVERSION                  FIELD-ALIGNED REMESHING
   (Blender Decimate: Collapse)        (Blender Decimate: Un-Subdivide)      (Quad Remesher / Instant Meshes)
           |                                      |                                      |
  - Pair contraction $(v_1, v_2) \to \bar{v}$    - Reverses subdivision passes           - Cross-field curvature alignment
  - Error quadrics $Q = \sum K_p$                - Preserves pure quad edge loops        - Global parameterization
  - Arbitrary triangle topology                  - Preserves UV grid structure           - Even quad density distribution
  - Optimal for LODs & static props              - Optimal for subdivided basemeshes     - Optimal for sculpted characters
```

## 2. Quadric Error Metric (QEM) Decimation Mathematics

### 2.1 Theoretical Foundation: Garland & Heckbert (SIGGRAPH 1997)
Seminal Literature: Michael Garland and Paul S. Heckbert, *"Surface Simplification Using Quadric Error Metrics"*, ACM SIGGRAPH 1997 (`doi:10.1145/258734.258849`).

#### Mathematical Derivation of the Fundamental Error Quadric
Let a triangle face plane $p$ be defined in Hessian normal form:
$$p = \begin{bmatrix} a \\ b \\ c \\ d \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{n} \\ d \end{bmatrix}, \quad \text{where } \|\mathbf{n}\| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2} = 1$$
The signed Euclidean distance from any arbitrary vertex $v = [x, y, z, 1]^T \in \mathbb{R}^4$ in homogeneous coordinates to the plane $p$ is:
$$D(v) = p^T v = ax + by + cz + d$$

The squared distance $D^2(v)$ is expressed as a quadratic form:
$$D^2(v) = (p^T v)^2 = (v^T p)(p^T v) = v^T (p p^T) v$$

The symmetric fundamental error quadric matrix $K_p \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 4}$ for plane $p$ is:
$$K_p = p p^T = \begin{bmatrix}
a^2 & ab & ac & ad \\
ab & b^2 & bc & bd \\
ac & bc & c^2 & cd \\
ad & bd & cd & d^2
\end{bmatrix}$$

For a vertex $v$, its total error quadric $Q(v)$ is the sum of fundamental quadrics of all incident faces $\mathcal{F}(v)$, weighted optionally by face area $A_f$:
$$Q(v) = \sum_{f \in \mathcal{F}(v)} A_f K_p$$

Partitioning $Q$ into a $3 \times 3$ symmetric tensor $\mathbf{A}$, a $3 \times 1$ column vector $\mathbf{b}$, and a scalar $c$:
$$Q = \begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{A} & \mathbf{b} \\
\mathbf{b}^T & c
\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}
q_{11} & q_{12} & q_{13} & q_{14} \\
q_{12} & q_{22} & q_{23} & q_{24} \\
q_{13} & q_{23} & q_{33} & q_{34} \\
q_{14} & q_{24} & q_{34} & q_{44}
\end{bmatrix}$$

Evaluating the geometric error metric $\Delta(v)$ for any spatial coordinate $\mathbf{v} = [x, y, z]^T$:
$$\Delta(v) = v^T Q v = \mathbf{v}^T \mathbf{A} \mathbf{v} + 2 \mathbf{b}^T \mathbf{v} + c$$

### 2.2 Pair Contraction and Optimal Vertex Placement
The fundamental atomic operation in QEM is the edge contraction $(v_1, v_2) \to \bar{v}$, which removes edge $(v_1, v_2)$, eliminates two adjacent triangles, and places a new vertex $\bar{v}$.

The error quadric for the new vertex is additive:
$$\bar{Q} = Q_1 + Q_2 = \begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{A}_1 + \mathbf{A}_2 & \mathbf{b}_1 + \mathbf{b}_2 \\
(\mathbf{b}_1 + \mathbf{b}_2)^T & c_1 + c_2
\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}
\bar{\mathbf{A}} & \bar{\mathbf{b}} \\
\bar{\mathbf{b}}^T & \bar{c}
\end{bmatrix}$$

To find the optimal position $\bar{\mathbf{v}} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ that minimizes the error $\Delta(\bar{v})$, we take the gradient with respect to $\bar{\mathbf{v}}$ and set it to zero:
$$\nabla \Delta(\bar{\mathbf{v}}) = 2 \bar{\mathbf{A}} \bar{\mathbf{v}} + 2 \bar{\mathbf{b}} = \mathbf{0} \implies \bar{\mathbf{A}} \bar{\mathbf{v}} = -\bar{\mathbf{b}}$$

In homogeneous matrix representation:
$$\begin{bmatrix}
q_{11} & q_{12} & q_{13} & q_{14} \\
q_{12} & q_{22} & q_{23} & q_{24} \\
q_{13} & q_{23} & q_{33} & q_{34} \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
\bar{x} \\ \bar{y} \\ \bar{z} \\ 1
\end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix}
0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 1
\end{bmatrix}$$

#### Singularity Handling (Degenerate Inversion):
If $\det(\bar{\mathbf{A}}) \approx 0$ (which occurs when all incident planes are nearly parallel, such as on a flat plane or cylindrical extrusion), $\bar{\mathbf{A}}$ is non-invertible. The decimation engine falls back to evaluating the cost at candidate endpoints:
$$\bar{\mathbf{v}} = \arg \min_{\mathbf{u} \in \{v_1, v_2, \frac{v_1 + v_2}{2}\}} \Delta(\mathbf{u})$$

### 2.3 Boundary & Constraint Penalties
To prevent the erosion of open mesh boundaries (e.g., sleeves, necklines, eye sockets), Garland & Heckbert introduce boundary constraint planes. For an open edge $e = (v_1, v_2)$ with incident face normal $\mathbf{n}$, a boundary plane $p_{\text{bound}}$ is constructed perpendicular to the face:
$$\mathbf{n}_{\text{bound}} = \frac{(v_2 - v_1) \times \mathbf{n}}{\|(v_2 - v_1) \times \mathbf{n}\|}, \quad d_{\text{bound}} = -\mathbf{n}_{\text{bound}} \cdot v_1$$
The fundamental quadric $K_{\text{bound}} = p_{\text{bound}} p_{\text{bound}}^T$ is multiplied by a user penalty weight $w_{\text{bound}} \approx 10^3 - 10^5$ and accumulated into $Q(v_1)$ and $Q(v_2)$. This heavily penalizes any contraction that distorts the silhouette or boundary curve.

## 3. Blender Decimate Modifier Implementation Analysis

In Blender's C++ codebase (`source/blender/modifiers/intern/MOD_decimate.cc` and `source/blender/blenkernel/intern/mesh_decimate_collapse.cc`), the Decimate modifier exposes three distinct algorithmic pathways:

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                  BLENDER DECIMATE MODIFIER MODES                                  |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  MODE 1: COLLAPSE                 |  MODE 2: UN-SUBDIVIDE             |  MODE 3: PLANAR           |
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+---------------------------+
|  - Engine: QEM Edge Collapse      |  - Engine: Grid Dual Inversion    |  - Engine: Face Dissolve  |
|  - Input: Arbitrary Triangles     |  - Input: Grid-structured Quads   |  - Input: Coplanar Polys  |
|  - Triangulates all quads         |  - Strict even-iteration collapse |  - Produces complex n-gons|
|  - Weight Group Modulation        |  - Preserves UVs & loop flow      |  - Delimited by UV/Normal |
|  - Symmetry Plane Mirroring       |  - Destroys odd-valence zones     |  - Flat hard-surface only |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 3.1 Mode 1: Collapse Mechanics
- **Internal Triangulation:** The algorithm internally converts all polygons to triangles before building the initial edge priority queue.
- **Priority Queue Execution:** Edges are stored in a min-heap sorted by contraction cost $\Delta(\bar{v})$. In each step, the cheapest valid edge is popped and contracted, and neighboring quadric matrices are updated.
- **Vertex Group Modulation:** Blender supports weighting the decimation error metric by a vertex group:
  $$\Delta_{\text{weighted}}(v) = \Delta(v) \cdot \left( 1.0 + \alpha \cdot W(v) \right)$$
  Where $W(v) \in [0.0, 1.0]$ is the vertex group weight, and $\alpha$ is the influence factor. Setting high weights on the face and hands of a character freezes edge collapse in those critical regions, forcing the modifier to extract 90% of its polygon reduction from the torso and legs.
- **Symmetry Preservation:** When enabled along an axis (e.g., $X$), contractions on the positive hemisphere ($+x$) are mirrored identically to the negative hemisphere ($-x$). If an edge crosses the symmetry plane, the optimal vertex $\bar{v}$ is constrained to lie strictly on the plane ($x = 0$).

### 3.2 Mode 2: Un-Subdivide Mechanics
- **Algorithmic Core:** Specifically inverts the Catmull-Clark and simple subdivision patterns.
- **Topological Invariant:** Requires vertices to possess a regular grid structure (valence 4). Every iteration $N$ reduces the polygon count by a factor of 4:
  $$F_{\text{out}} = \frac{F_{\text{in}}}{4^N}$$
- **Advantages:** Unlike Collapse, Un-Subdivide **preserves quad topology and continuous edge loops**. It does not introduce irregular poles or triangular triangulation artifacts, making it the ideal decimation tool for models that originated from subdivision surface modeling.

### 3.3 Mode 3: Planar Mechanics
- **Algorithmic Core:** Iteratively dissolves adjacent faces whose angle between face normal vectors falls below `Angle Limit` $\theta_{\text{limit}}$ (e.g., $1.5^\circ - 5.0^\circ$).
- **Delimiters:** Allows artists to lock edges based on `Normal`, `Material`, `Seam`, or `Sharp` boundaries.
- **Output:** Outputs large planar n-gons. Not suitable for deforming characters, but highly effective for CAD, architectural, and hard-surface vehicle meshes.

## 4. Retopology Workflows: Algorithmic Remeshing vs. Manual Retopology

### 4.1 Quad Remesher / Mixed-Integer Quadrangulation (Exoside / Bommes et al. 2009)
Production character pipelines frequently employ field-aligned remeshers such as **Quad Remesher** (based on Mixed-Integer Quadrangulation; Bommes et al., SIGGRAPH 2009, `doi:10.1145/1618452.1618523`):
1. **Cross-Field Computation:** Computes a smooth 4-RoSy (4-direction rotationally symmetric) tangent field aligned with principal curvature directions across the surface.
2. **Singularity Isolation:** Determines the positions of extraordinary poles ($k = 3, 5$) by analyzing the index of the vector field.
3. **Global Parameterization:** Solves a mixed-integer linear program to generate seamless 2D parameterization charts whose integer isolines define the final edge loops.
4. **Result:** Produces a uniform, highly regular quad mesh that aligns with muscle contours, vastly outperforming raw QEM decimation in terms of deformation capability.

### 4.2 Manual Retopology vs. Automated Remeshing Comparison

| Evaluation Metric | Manual Retopology | Quad Remesher (MIQ) | Instant Meshes | QEM Collapse Decimate |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Topology Type** | Pure Quads (100%) | Semi-Pure Quads ($>98\%$) | Pure Quads ($\sim 95\%$) | Triangles (Triangulated) |
| **Edge Loop Flow** | 100% Anatomical alignment | Curvature-aligned | Curvature-aligned | Uncontrolled / Random |
| **Pole Placement** | Hand-routed to neutral zones | Algorithmic (singularities) | Loose / Irregular | Chaotic extraordinary poles |
| **Time to Author** | 4 to 12 hours per hero asset | 15 to 45 seconds | 10 to 30 seconds | Instantaneous (Modifier) |
| **Deformability Score** | $10 / 10$ (Zero pinching) | $8 / 10$ (Good for body) | $5 / 10$ (Spiral loops) | $2 / 10$ (Catastrophic pinch) |
| **Optimal Pipeline Stage**| Hero Deforming Characters | Background Characters, Props | Rapid Prototyping | Distance LODs (LOD1 - LOD3) |

## 5. Normal Map & Displacement Baking Mechanics

When a high-resolution sculpt ($5\text{M} - 20\text{M}$ polygons) is simplified to a real-time low-poly base mesh ($15\text{k} - 50\text{k}$ polygons), surface curvature and micro-detail are reconstructed using normal maps and displacement maps.

```
                           TANGENT SPACE NORMAL BAKING (MikkTSpace)
    High-Poly Surface               +--------------------------------------+
    (True Geometry)                 |   o       o   High-Freq Sculpt Detail|
                                    +--/---------\-------------------------+
                                      /     ^     \   Ray Cast Intersection
                                     /      |      \
    Baking Cage                     +-------|-------+ (Extruded along Averaged Normals)
                                    |       |       |
    Low-Poly Surface                +-------+-------+ 
    (Interpolated Tangent Frame)            v_i
                                  [T, B, N] Orthogonal Frame
```

### 5.1 Tangent-Space vs. Object-Space Normal Maps

#### Tangent-Space Normal Maps (TSNM):
Normals are encoded relative to the local surface coordinate frame formed by the tangent $\mathbf{T}$, bitangent $\mathbf{B}$, and normal $\mathbf{N}$:
$$\mathbf{n}_{\text{world}} = \mathbf{T} \cdot n_x + \mathbf{B} \cdot n_y + \mathbf{N} \cdot n_z$$
- **Encoded Values:** $[0, 0, 1]$ (pointing straight out) maps to RGB color $[128, 128, 255]$ (periwinkle blue).
- **Deformation Compatibility:** **Mandatory for skinned characters.** Because normals are defined relative to the local surface, as the skeletal armature bends, the tangent frame rotates dynamically, preserving correct surface shading under animation.

#### Object-Space Normal Maps (OSNM):
Normals are encoded in the global/object coordinate space:
$$\mathbf{n}_{\text{object}} = [n_x, n_y, n_z]^T \in [-1, 1]^3 \implies \text{RGB} = \frac{\mathbf{n} + 1}{2} \cdot 255$$
- **Limitation:** Cannot be used on deforming skinned meshes. If a character bends its arm, object-space normals remain fixed in object space, causing lighting to invert and shatter.

### 5.2 The MikkTSpace Standard (Mikkelsen 2008 / 2020)
Seminal Reference: Morten Mikkelsen, *"Simulation of Wrinkled Surfaces Revisited"*, 2008.

#### Why Tangent Synchronization is Critical:
If the Digital Content Creation tool (Blender) and the real-time engine (Three.js, Unreal Engine, Unity) calculate vertex tangents and bitangents using even slightly different formulas, the reconstructed normal vector in the pixel shader will diverge:
$$\mathbf{n}_{\text{reconstructed}} = \mathbf{T}_{\text{engine}} \cdot n_x + \mathbf{B}_{\text{engine}} \cdot n_y + \mathbf{N}_{\text{engine}} \cdot n_z \ne \mathbf{n}_{\text{original}}$$
This divergence manifests as visible dark seams along UV borders and inverted specular highlights.
- **The Solution:** The **MikkTSpace** standard defines a deterministic, cross-platform algorithm for generating vertex tangents based on face-weighted angles and UV derivatives. Blender bakes using MikkTSpace natively (`source/blender/blenkernel/intern/mikktspace.c`), and Three.js glTF loaders calculate tangents via `BufferGeometryUtils.computeMikkTSpaceTangents()`.

### 5.3 Cage Extrusion Mathematics
During normal map baking, low-poly faces cast rays inward to sample high-poly normals. On hard edges or curved surfaces, un-caged ray casting misses geometry or creates overlap artifacts.
- A **Baking Cage** is an exact topological clone of the low-poly mesh whose vertices are extruded outward along averaged vertex normals $\bar{\mathbf{N}}_v$:
  $$v_{\text{cage}} = v_{\text{low}} + d_{\text{push}} \cdot \bar{\mathbf{N}}_v$$
- Rays are cast inward from $v_{\text{cage}}$ toward $v_{\text{low}}$. Because vertex count and indexing are 1:1 identical, ray direction is continuous across seams, eliminating baking gaps and projection seams.
