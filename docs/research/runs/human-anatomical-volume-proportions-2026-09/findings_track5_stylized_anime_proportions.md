## 1. Classical Realistic Proportions: The Canons of Human Proportion

In figurative art, human morphology has historically been systematized using the vertical height of the head (cranial vertex to menton/chin, denoted $1H$) as a modular unit.

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    HISTORICAL PROPORTION CANONS                                       |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. Polykleitos (Doryphoros, c. 440 BCE): 7.0 heads tall. Natural athletic proportion.                  |
| 2. Lysippos (Apoxyomenos, c. 330 BCE):  8.0 heads tall. Smaller head, longer limbs, graceful presence. |
| 3. Vitruvius (De Architectura, c. 15 BCE): 8.0 heads tall. Midpoint at pubis; navel at center of circle.|
| 4. Gottfried Bammes (1964, 1990):        7.5 heads tall. Measured medical/anatomical average adult.   |
| 5. Andrew Loomis (1943):                 7.5 heads (Normal), 8.0 heads (Heroic), 9.0 heads (Fashion).  |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1.1 Andrew Loomis 8-Head Heroic Canon (1943)
Andrew Loomis (*Figure Drawing for All It's Worth*, 1943) established the standard 8-head grid for commercial illustration and heroic character design:

| Landmark Line | Head Height Division | Anatomical Landmark & Spatial Alignment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Top of Head** | $0.0H$ ($0.0\%$ height) | Cranial vertex (superior skull surface). |
| **Line 1** | $1.0H$ ($12.5\%$ height) | Inferior border of the chin (menton). |
| **Line 2** | $2.0H$ ($25.0\%$ height) | Nipple line (mid-sternum / 4th intercostal space). |
| **Line 3** | $3.0H$ ($37.5\%$ height) | Navel (umbilicus) / lower costal margin (10th rib). |
| **Line 4** | **$4.0H$ ($50.0\%$ height)**| **Pubic symphysis (anatomical midpoint of the standing human figure).** |
| **Line 5** | $5.0H$ ($62.5\%$ height) | Lower border of the gluteus maximus / mid-thigh. |
| **Line 6** | $6.0H$ ($75.0\%$ height) | Inferior margin of the patella (knee joint line). |
| **Line 7** | $7.0H$ ($87.5\%$ height) | Lower belly of the gastrocnemius muscle (mid-calf). |
| **Line 8** | $8.0H$ ($100.0\%$ height)| Plantar surface of the foot (sole / heel contact). |

#### Critical Anatomical Invariant:
The true vertical midpoint of an adult human is the **pubic symphysis** ($4.0H / 8.0H = 0.500$), **not the waist or navel**. The distance from the cranial vertex to the pubic crest equals the distance from the pubic crest to the sole of the foot ($4H : 4H$).

### 1.2 Gottfried Bammes Anatomical Standard (1964, 1990)
Gottfried Bammes (*Die Gestalt des Menschen*, Dresden) measured anatomical averages across European populations:
- Real human adults average **$7.0 \text{ to } 7.5$ head heights**.
- Shoulder width across the acromion processes equals $2.0$ head heights in males ($1.5 - 1.6H$ in females).
- Pelvic bi-iliac width equals $1.5$ head heights in females ($1.2 - 1.3H$ in males).

## 3. Systematic Anatomical Deformations in Anime Character Art

When translating realistic 3D human meshes to anime/stylized aesthetics, character modelers execute two deliberate structural shifts:

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                            ANIME VOLUMETRIC TRANSFORMATION PROFILE                                |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. Torso Volume Compression:       -15% to -25% reduction in anteroposterior depth & ribcage bulk.|
| 2. Distal Limb Elongation:         +10% to +25% vertical stretch of forearms, shanks, and feet.   |
| 3. Extremity Magnification:        +15% to +35% cross-sectional enlargement of hands and feet.     |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 3.1 Torso Volume Compression ($-15\% \text{ to } -25\%$)
- **Morphological Mechanism:**
  - The anteroposterior (AP) chest depth is reduced by $20\% - 30\%$ relative to transverse shoulder breadth, transforming an oval ribcage cross section into a flat, cylindrical slab.
  - The abdominal waist is narrowed ("wasp waist" taper), removing the anatomical flare of the external obliques and erector spinae.
- **Volumetric Impact:**
  Because the torso accounts for **$43.46\%$ of human body mass** and $\approx 47\%$ of volume in realistic biology, a $20\%$ compression of torso volume removes approximately **$8.7\%$ to $10.0\%$ of total body mass**, resulting in an unnaturally low total body mass if limb volumes are held constant.

### 3.2 Distal Limb Elongation & Extremity Magnification ($+10\% \text{ to } +35\%$)
- **Kinetic Readability Justification (Arc System Works GDC 2015, Studio Trigger):**
  - In fast 2D/3D action sequences ($12 - 24\text{ fps}$), realistic hands and feet appear tiny, cluttering the central silhouette and obscuring weapon grasps, martial blocks, and footwork.
  - Elongating the shanks/calves by $+15\%$ to $+25\%$ lowers the apparent camera vanishing point, emphasizing ground contact and stride length.
  - Enlarging the hands (fists) and footwear (boots) by $+20\%$ to $+35\%$ creates bold, readable silhouettes that track cleanly across high-velocity motion blur and dynamic screen space.

## 4. Volumetric Scaling Laws & Combat Physics Translation

Translating stylized character models into physically plausible simulation engines requires strict mathematical accounting of scaling laws.

### 4.1 The 3D Volume Scaling Law
Under affine 3D scaling by scale factors $\mathbf{s} = [s_x, s_y, s_z]^T$:
$$\boxed{V(\mathbf{s}) = s_x \cdot s_y \cdot s_z \cdot V_0}$$

#### Case A: Isometric Uniform Scaling
If a limb is scaled uniformly in all three dimensions by factor $s = 1.25$ ($+25\%$ size):
$$V = s^3 V_0 = (1.25)^3 V_0 \approx 1.953 \cdot V_0 \quad (\mathbf{+95.3\%\text{ volume ballooning!}})$$
Uniformly enlarging an anime character's hands or boots by $25\%$ nearly **doubles their mass**, catastrophic for realistic combat simulation!

#### Case B: Non-Uniform Volume-Preserving Ellipsoidal Scaling
To elongate a limb along its longitudinal axis ($z$) by $+20\%$ ($s_z = 1.20$) while preserving true biological volume ($V / V_0 = 1.0$):
$$s_x \cdot s_y \cdot s_z = 1.0 \implies s_x \cdot s_y = \frac{1}{1.20} \approx 0.833$$
Assuming symmetric transverse narrowing ($s_x = s_y = s_\perp$):
$$s_\perp = \sqrt{\frac{1}{1.20}} \approx 0.913 \quad (-8.7\%\text{ transverse radius})$$
By slimming the limb diameter by just $8.7\%$, a character artist can achieve a dramatic $20\%$ elongation with **zero unphysical mass ballooning**.

### 4.2 Impact Physics and Rotational Inertia Distortion ($I = m k^2$)
In combat mechanics (punches, roundhouse kicks, sword swings), the difficulty of accelerating a limb is governed by its moment of inertia around the joint rotation axis (shoulder or hip):
$$I_{\text{joint}} = I_{\text{CoM}} + m d^2 \approx m k_{\text{prox}}^2$$
where $k_{\text{prox}}$ is the radius of gyration relative to the proximal joint.

```
Realistic Human Arm:                 [Shoulder]======(Elbow)======[Hand]
                                     Mass concentrated proximally; low rotational inertia.

Uncorrected Stylized Arm:            [Shoulder]======(Elbow)======[BIG FIST (+95% Mass)]
                                     Distal mass center shifts outward; I increases by >150%!
```

#### Dynamic Consequences of Uncorrected Distal Scaling:
1. **Inertia Explosion:** Enlarging distal hands and feet without density correction shifts the limb center of mass outward. Because $I \propto m \cdot r^2$, a $50\%$ increase in distal hand mass paired with a $20\%$ longer forearm increases arm rotational inertia $I_{\text{shoulder}}$ by:
   $$\frac{I'}{I} \approx \frac{m'}{m} \cdot \left(\frac{r'}{r}\right)^2 = (1.50) \cdot (1.20)^2 = 1.50 \cdot 1.44 = \mathbf{2.16} \quad (\mathbf{+116\%\text{ increase!}})$$
2. **Torque Deficit:** The virtual character's simulated musculature would require **$2.16\times$ higher joint torque** to swing a punch at identical angular velocity, making strikes appear sluggish and un-snappy.
3. **Engineering Solution:** In the physics engine, character rigs with stylized proportions must assign a **compensatory virtual density reduction** to distal extremities:
   $$\rho_{\text{fist}} = \frac{V_{\text{anatomical}}}{V_{\text{stylized}}} \rho_{\text{muscle}} \approx 0.50 \times 1.06\text{ g/cm}^3 \approx 0.53\text{ g/cm}^3$$
   preserving the true multi-body mass balance, strike kinetic energy ($E_k = \frac{1}{2} I \omega^2$), and dynamic equilibrium.
