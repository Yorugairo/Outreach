Stock market analysis that answers the charts everyone is sharing. First up: the AI bubble.

This playlist is the reply desk. Every episode takes one live market call from a named source - the chart, the thesis, the market news going around - and underwrites it. We start with the strongest version of their case. Then we run our own numbers underneath it: primary sources, our own tooling, customs and shipment data, grid and capacity deliverables - the aspects that can't lie. Then we call it. Right when they're right. Wrong when they're wrong. And we show the work either way.

Episode one answers Bravos Research - credit where due, the sharpest macro chart on YouTube - and their warning that AI is 1845 again. We agree the cycle is real. We disagree about where the warning points, and we prove it with the yardstick their chart implies but never draws: computing and software as a share of every private investment dollar in America, straight from the government's own statisticians. Twenty-three cents at the dot-com peak. Twenty-eight today. Nothing since the railways has matched the railways, and this is the closest run at it in a hundred and eighty years.

What every reply in this playlist does:
- Steelmans the source at full strength before contesting a single number.
- Runs the yardstick: the measure the chart needs but never shows.
- Names the tripwire: the specific rate, ratio, or date that flips the call.
- Sorts the holdings into steel or paper: what survives the cycle, and what was only ever a promise.
- Ends with a tell you can check yourself: one variable, one threshold, where we sit now, and what flips us.

Standing subjects, because the market keeps handing them to us: the AI bubble and hyperscaler capex; semiconductor and memory stocks (DRAM, HBM, and the wafer math behind them); Fed interest rates, bond yields, and what the credit market says that the stock market won't; market crash history - the 1840s railway mania, the 2000 dot-com peak, and what each collapse actually left standing; index concentration and what your index fund is really holding; and the borrowing behind the AI buildout - the bonds, the lease commitments, and the cash-flow arithmetic the spending quietly outgrew.

The terms, in plain words, because a chart you can't read is a chart you can't check:
- AI capex: what the largest technology companies spend building data centers, chips, and power - the biggest investment wave since the railways.
- HBM and DRAM: the memory inside every AI accelerator. HBM stacks the dies, which is why one gigabyte of it eats the wafer capacity of three gigabytes of standard DRAM.
- Memory contract prices: what the big buyers actually pay each quarter - the earliest hard signal of whether AI demand is real.
- The yardstick: technology's share of all private investment - the one measure that puts 1845, 2000, and today on the same line.
- The tripwire: the interest rate at which past manias rolled over. The Bank of England above six percent in the 1840s. The Fed at six and a half in 2000.
- Investment-grade credit weight: how much of the bond index is now technology - the quiet way data-center debt lands in your bond fund.
- Steel or paper: the sort. Steel is capacity that keeps working through a crash. Paper is a promise priced as if it had already been kept.

Sources we grade in public: TrendForce, SemiAnalysis, Fabricated Knowledge, Cappy Army, Bravos Research - the best in the business of finance, economics, and geopolitics operating in the open. We cite them. We don't gatekeep them. And we measure our own work against theirs.
