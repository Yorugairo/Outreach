Mitsuyo Maeda arrived in Brazil carrying everything Jigoro Kano had built at the Kodokan. What the world called judo, Maeda still fought as jiu-jitsu, and in the vale tudo rings he proved it worked. Carlos Gracie watched, learned, and taught his brothers, and the youngest, Helio, rebuilt the art around leverage. Decades later a kimura would break an arm in Rio, an americana would win a world title, and the Gracie name would carry Kano's ideas further than the Kodokan ever imagined.
