Trump wants America to win the AI race. Anthropic's Dario Amodei wants a slowdown. But what if the fastest way forward is fixing what we've already built? You ask AI to finish a job. Seconds later, the screen fills up. Looks finished. Then you find the wrong number. The missing source. The file it never saved. Now its finished job is your afternoon. Imagine each step works nineteen times out of twenty. Sounds dependable. Chain twenty steps together, and only about four jobs out of ten finish without a mistake. The other six land back on your desk. Now push each step to ninety-nine percent. About eight out of ten finish cleanly. Roughly twice as many finished jobs. Same ten waiting customers.

That's where I see the economic opportunity. A shop owner could send the quote and take the next customer. An accountant could finish the return instead of hunting through invented receipts. Multiply those recovered hours across businesses, and reliability starts looking like an economic engine. Amodei's argument is about safety, including better testing and operations. I think that work could pay a productivity dividend too. Of course, a stronger model might make fewer mistakes. So put both approaches on the same job. Count how often you have to step in. If fixing today's AI saves more human time, slowing the frontier could speed up the economy. Winning the AI race should give you your afternoon back.
