No claim that any actual product has95% or99% reliability, or that memory/tooling delivers this exact improvement. Correlated failures, retries, checks, costs and task differences change real outcomes.

## Our argument, not established causality
If practical failure rates improve, more identical workflows finish cleanly. Resource allocation toward memory, tool integration and checking could improve reliability; this is the thesis to argue, not a measured causal result of a development pause. Stronger base models can also improve reliability. Slowing frontier development without productive reallocation offers no demonstrated benefit in this argument.

Supporting economic framework, not pause experiment: https://www.nber.org/papers/w25148 and https://www.nber.org/papers/w24001 discuss complementary organizational investment and delayed productivity effects. Do not use them to assert a proven slowdown dividend.
