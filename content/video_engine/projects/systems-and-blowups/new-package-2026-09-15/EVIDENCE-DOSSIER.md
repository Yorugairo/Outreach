# Evidence dossier — refinancing lag candidate

Status: draft review, not approved. No current US debt or interest totals asserted. This is a controlled example explaining fixed-coupon refinancing, not a forecast of total budget interest.

## Primary mechanism checked by parent
TreasuryDirect, Treasury Notes: https://www.treasurydirect.gov/marketable-securities/treasury-notes/ — read 2026-09-15. Notes have a fixed rate until maturity; payment is semiannual. The line about Treasury notes concerns their coupon, not a changing secondary-market yield.

TreasuryDirect, Understanding Pricing and Interest Rates: https://www.treasurydirect.gov/marketable-securities/understanding-pricing/ — read 2026-09-15. Coupon and yield are different; issue price can differ from par. The simplified example assumes par issuance and annualized coupon expense, no discount/premium amortization.

## Caveats to keep in source notes and framing
Fixed principal, fixed coupons, one refinance at par. Actual Treasury financing includes bills, floating-rate notes, TIPS, new deficits, different maturities and issue prices. The result shows how costs CAN rise after a new rate falls, not that rate cuts cause larger bills or every security behaves this way. No investment recommendation.
