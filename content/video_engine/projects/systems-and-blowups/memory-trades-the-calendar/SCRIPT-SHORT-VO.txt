Memory stocks don't trade the news. They trade the calendar. [post-key]

Around the fifteenth of every month, Korean customs prints the price of memory chips crossing its border. If you own the stocks, that print is your metronome. Hynix and Micron move around it, not after it.

[rehook] But here's the part the headline hides: when does the month's gain actually land? Twenty-five months of prints, August twenty-four to August twenty-six. [new] In the half-month into the print, the stocks average plus six point eight percent. In the half-month after: plus two point two. The move is front-run.

[new] A strong print? Already paid for. After the fourteen strong prints, the next two weeks averaged plus three point four. Eight of fourteen up. A coin flip with drift.

[rehook] [catalyst] But now the weak prints. Eight of them in twenty-five months. After seven of the eight, the stocks fell. Average: minus six point nine.

[new] The latest landed in July. One soft print, and the stocks dropped fourteen percent in two weeks. The chip price itself? Sixteen percent higher a month later.

In finance, we call this asymmetry. Good news is priced before it lands. Bad news is the only surprise left.

So you don't have to read the print faster than the market. [ring] You have to know the calendar: the day the print lands is the day the stocks trade it.
