# Tokyo Tea Break — the close, rewritten (2026-09-03)

What the old close did wrong, against the exemplars: the ring token ("tea break")
appeared once and never returned; there was no tell — the line named the variable
and stopped; it ended on an explanation ("aren't coming back down") instead of a
landing; and it spent its last breath on cable news, the opponent's frame, not ours.

The rewrite keeps everything above "So Tokyo took a tea break." Every figure is
spoken as words (doc 37 §4). Tags are the kit's vocabulary only — `tell` and
`assignment` are not marks, and an unknown mark is SPOKEN by the recorder. The tell
uses the doctrine's labelled form (*variable · threshold · where we sit · the flip*),
which is the Script B exemplar and what the audit recognises; the flip includes being
wrong out loud. The ring closes LAST and returns the **opening image** — the Fed
that "didn't move an inch" — transformed: it still hasn't.

## What the tell is NOT claiming

The threshold is arithmetic, not a statistic: the trade is underwater when the hedge
costs more than the bond pays. Five-and-a-half and five are the script's own figures
and stay under SOURCES-TO-VERIFY with a date — the tell inherits that, it does not
add to it. "Tokyo comes back" is the flip the viewer can watch for at the next
auction, which is the point of a tell.
