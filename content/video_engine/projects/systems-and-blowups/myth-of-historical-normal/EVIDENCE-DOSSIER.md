# EVIDENCE DOSSIER — The Myth of "Historical Normal"

## Core Thesis
A nominal interest rate is not an anchor; it is a weight on a balance sheet. The Wall Street talking point that "5% or 6% yields are historically normal" ignores the mechanical law that leverage dictates carrying capacity. An economy at 31% debt-to-GDP and 7.5x P/E multiples absorbed 15% yields because capital duration was short, debt service was minimal, and earnings yields were 13.3%. Today, at 122% debt-to-GDP, 25x multiples, and near-zero equity risk premium, the identical 5.5% nominal yield is a mechanical vice.

## Verified Data Series & Citations

1. **US 10-Year Treasury Constant Maturity (`DGS10` / FRED)**
   - September 30, 1981: 15.84% (All-time peak)
   - January 20, 2000: 6.79% (Dot-Com cycle peak, real yield > +4.0%)
   - May 2000 Fed Funds Target: 6.50%
   - March 2020: 0.54% (All-time low)
   - 2023-2026 Range: 4.20% – 5.02%

2. **S&P 500 Valuation & Earnings Yield (Robert Shiller / S&P Dow Jones / FRED)**
   - August 1982: P/E 7.5x -> Earnings Yield ($1 / \text{P/E}$) = 13.33%
   - March 2000: P/E 29.4x (Operating) / 44x (CAPE) -> Earnings Yield = 2.27% - 3.40%
   - Modern (2024-2026): P/E ~24.5x -> Earnings Yield = 4.08%
   - Equity Risk Premium (ERP = S&P Earnings Yield - 10Y UST Yield):
     - 1982: -2.5% vs 15.8% (extreme Volcker crunch, but rapidly expanded to +5.0% as rates plunged)
     - 2000: -3.4% to -4.5% (extreme overvaluation before 78% Nasdaq collapse)
     - 2024-2026: -0.5% to +0.2% (lowest equity risk premium in 22 years)

3. **US Federal Debt & Interest Service (FRED / US Treasury)**
   - 1981 Federal Debt-to-GDP (`GFDEGDQ188S`): 31.1% ($998 Billion total debt)
   - 2024-2026 Federal Debt-to-GDP: 122.4% ($34.5+ Trillion total debt)
   - Federal Net Outlays on Interest (`A091RC1Q027SBEA`):
     - 1981: ~$71 Billion
     - 2024-2026: Exceeding $1.05 Trillion annualized
     - Comparison: Exceeds total US National Defense discretionary budget (~$850 Billion)

4. **Corporate Maturity Wall & Debt Service (Federal Reserve Financial Accounts / Bloomberg / ICE BofA)**
   - 2020-2021: Investment Grade corporate debt issued at weighted average coupon of 2.4% - 3.1%
   - 2025-2027 Refinancing Wall: Over $2.2 Trillion in corporate debt maturing
   - Replacement Cost: New issuance yields between 5.8% and 7.1% (a 300-400 bps refinancing step-up)
   - Free Cash Flow Cannibalization: 15% to 25% drop in discretionary Capex for non-mega-cap corporate issuers
