# Research Findings: British Railway Mania Return Lag ("Twenty Years")

**Claim Under Review (Money Physics: *Steel and Paper*, Script H line 41):**
> *"Railway steel sat waiting twenty years while the economy caught up. That's why the return took two decades. Today's compute doesn't sit. It depreciates in about five years."*

**Verification Question:**
Is the "twenty years" / "two decades" lag between the 1840s British Railway Mania build-out (peak 1845–1847) and the economy/returns catching up supported by primary and academic sources?

## Verdict

SUPPORTED

*(Note for narration accuracy: The financial lag of 20 to 26 years is fully supported. Gareth Campbell explicitly notes that "During the subsequent two decades the rises in dividends were modest and rates barely returned to their pre-Mania levels," and Odlyzko confirms that the £560M capital and ~5% return projected in 1846 took 26 years to 1872. Narratively, note that the 1840s tracks were wrought iron rather than steel, as Bessemer steel rails were only adopted 20 years later in the mid-1860s to 1870s.)*
