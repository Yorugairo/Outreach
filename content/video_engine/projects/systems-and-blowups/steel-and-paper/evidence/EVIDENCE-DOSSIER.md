## A. Railway mania (1840s Britain)

**A1 — Peak railway investment ≈ 7% of British GDP.** VERIFIED.
Railway investment consumed roughly 7% of British GDP across 1844–47. At
peak, physical spending topped £40M annually — more than 6.5% of national
income and about 50% of the country's entire gross domestic capital
formation. Source: Campbell & Turner literature; Focus-Economics summary.
→ **Route: our chart.** The strongest number available to this episode,
and the script does not currently use it.

**A2 — The crash was 64.1%, not 70%.** ⚠ **SCRIPT FIX**
Script B says *"Then the stocks crashed seventy percent."*
Campbell & Turner's daily railway share index (the primary academic
series, 442 companies, 1843–1850): base 1,000 in January 1843 → peak
**2,062 on 6 October 1845** (+106.2%) → trough **741 in April 1850**.
That is a **64.1% peak-to-trough decline**, ending *below* its starting
value. It also fell 20.2% in the first month alone.
→ Narration must say "**more than sixty percent**" or "nearly
two-thirds." Do not ship "seventy percent."
→ **Route: our chart.** The index curve 1843–1850 is the single best
evidence document available for this episode.

**A3 — Parliament authorised roughly 3× what was built.** VERIFIED.
Across 1844–46 Parliament sanctioned bills for 8,470 miles of railway,
about three times the mileage actually constructed. In 1845 alone roughly
3,000 miles were authorised — comparable to the previous 15 years
combined. Source: Jackman (1916), p. 585.
→ Route: our chart (authorised vs built), or a badge on A2.

**A4 — Dividends followed prices down, late.** VERIFIED.
Railway dividends stayed high for about 18 months after prices turned,
then fell from 7% (1847) to 3% (1850). Supports the "paper priced off the
best year" beat directly.
