The AI bubble is real. Bravos' chart is right. The warning points at the wrong address.

Bravos Research drew the sharpest AI-bubble chart on YouTube: the mega-caps up 21% in a year, the chip industry up 105%, and the warning that AI is 1845 again. This video takes that chart at full strength - and then runs the numbers it never drew.

The yardstick Bravos didn't run: computing and software as a share of every private investment dollar in America, from the government's own statisticians. 23 cents at the dot-com peak. 28 cents today - the most it has ever been, and still short of the railways, which took half of everything Britain invested in the 1840s.

The tripwire that ends every mania: not age, rates. The Bank of England above 6% rolled the railways over. The Fed at 6.5% rolled the internet over. Bravos' line for this cycle is the Fed back above 5.5%. We agree, and we put it on the record.

Then the part no chart is drawing. The biggest builders paid for AI out of pocket until the bills outgrew the cash: about $28 billion a year of borrowing from 2020 to 2024, $121 billion last year, tracking toward $150 billion this year - plus $822 billion in data-center lease commitments already signed and not yet on a balance sheet. PIMCO has the buildout consuming 94 cents of every dollar these companies generate from operations. "Funded from profits" was true. It stopped being true about eighteen months ago.

And the layer Bravos' chart never had: memory - the builders inside the builders - up 613%.

So the question isn't when the disappointment arrives. It's arriving. The question is what survives. In 1849 it wasn't the paper. It was the steel. This video ends with a three-question test that sorts every holding you own into steel or paper. Thirty seconds a stock. Run it yourself.
