For years the giants paid for all this out of pocket. Cash on hand, no borrowing — that was the whole flex. Then the bills got bigger than the cash.

Between 2020 and 2024, the five of them borrowed about twenty-eight billion dollars a year, between them. Last year: a hundred and twenty-one billion. This year they're tracking toward a hundred and fifty.

That is not a rounding error in someone's capital structure. It is big enough to bend the bond market around it. Technology used to be about nine percent of the investment-grade index — the pool your bond fund buys from. It's ten now, and the projections run past twelve. Money that used to sit in utilities and banks is quietly becoming a bet on data centers.

And none of this is slowing down. When the year opened, the street had these five spending about four hundred and eighty billion between them. Consensus now says six hundred and ninety this year, and eight hundred and seventy next. The estimate went up faster than the year went by.

Go into the filings and there's another eight hundred and twenty-two billion in lease commitments. Data centers they've already agreed to pay for, on contracts already signed, that haven't landed on a balance sheet yet. Not hidden — the number is right there in the filing. Just not counted where anyone looks.

Put it together and the arithmetic gets tight. Over the next two years, building this is expected to consume ninety-four percent of every dollar these companies generate from operations. Ninety-four. That is not a company investing its surplus. That is a company spending all of it, and borrowing the difference.

So when you hear the buildout is funded out of profits — that was true. It stopped being true about eighteen months ago.

Nobody committed fraud here. They signed a promise, in a year that looked good. And promises are the part of this that nobody is charting.
